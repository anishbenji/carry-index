# Research notes: woven nylon and polyester bag fabrics

Scope: woven nylon and woven polyester bag fabrics, plus the coatings and finishes they share. Compiled 23 October 2026 with WebSearch and WebFetch only. The shared WebSearch budget ran out partway through, so later items rely on pages already found or on direct page fetches (see "Open questions / unreachable").

Conventions:
- "(conv.)" marks my own unit conversion, using 1 oz/yd² = 33.906 g/m².
- "Method not stated" means the source gives no test standard.
- "Maker claim" means a marketing statement with no test data.
- Nylon (section A) and polyester (section B) are kept separate for their two pages. Sections C and D cover both.

## Findings

### A. NYLON

#### A1. Chemistry and properties

**Polymer basics**
- Nylon 6,6 is named for its two six-carbon starting chemicals, adipic acid and hexamethylenediamine. Nylon 6 is made from caprolactam. [Britannica: Nylon](https://www.britannica.com/science/nylon)
- Nylons were developed in the 1930s by a DuPont research team led by Wallace Carothers. [Britannica: Nylon](https://www.britannica.com/science/nylon)
- Polyamides generally have a high degree of crystallinity. Nylon is drawn to about four times its starting length to orient its molecules. [Britannica: Nylon](https://www.britannica.com/science/nylon)
- The deep-dive's claims that silk and wool are "natural polyamides" and that amide bonds let chains "grip" each other were **not checked**. I opened no source on them.

**Melting point**
- Nylon 6,6 resin: 262 °C, by DSC per ISO 11357-3 at 10 °C/min (DuPont Zytel 101L NC010, datasheet created 3 May 2018). [Zytel 101L datasheet (DuPont, hosted by Protolabs)](https://Protolabs.com/media/tcebrq5j/zytel-101l.pdf)
- Nylon 6 resin: 220 °C, by DSC per ISO 11357-1/-3 (BASF Ultramid B3S HP R01, datasheet dated 09/2026). [BASF Ultramid B3S HP R01 datasheet](https://download.basf.com/p1/8a80825f8ec1f32b018ee0f5a5bf6e14/en/Ultramid%25C2%25AE_B3S_HP_R01_UNCOLORED)
- A conservation-science materials database gives one undifferentiated figure for nylon fibre: 250 °C. [CAMEO (MFA Boston): Nylon fiber](https://cameo.mfa.org/wiki/Nylon_fiber)
- A rope maker's fibre table gives nylon at 425 °F (218 °C), which fits nylon 6. The type is not stated. [Unirope TB21 fiber characteristics](https://www.unirope.com/wp-content/themes/unirope/pdfs/TB21_k-spec_fiber_characteristics.pdf)

**Density**
- Nylon 6,6 resin: 1.14 g/cm³ per ISO 1183. [Zytel 101L datasheet](https://Protolabs.com/media/tcebrq5j/zytel-101l.pdf)
- Nylon fibre: 1.14 g/ml. [CAMEO: Nylon fiber](https://cameo.mfa.org/wiki/Nylon_fiber)
- Nylon specific gravity: 1.14 (Unirope). [Unirope TB21](https://www.unirope.com/wp-content/themes/unirope/pdfs/TB21_k-spec_fiber_characteristics.pdf)

**Moisture regain at standard air conditions**
- Fibre moisture regain: nylon 6 2.8–5.0 %, nylon 6,6 4.0–4.5 %. The table cites Kadolph (1998) and does not state conditions. [Wikipedia: Yarn conditioning (table from Kadolph 1998)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarn_conditioning)
- CAMEO gives nylon fibre regain as 4.2–5.0 %. [CAMEO: Nylon fiber](https://cameo.mfa.org/wiki/Nylon_fiber)
- CAMEO defines regain as water uptake by dried material at 65 % RH and 22 °C, expressed as a percentage of dry weight. [CAMEO: Moisture regain](https://cameo.mfa.org/wiki/Moisture_regain)
- Unirope lists nylon regain as 4.0–6.0 %. [Unirope TB21](https://www.unirope.com/wp-content/themes/unirope/pdfs/TB21_k-spec_fiber_characteristics.pdf)
- The standard table of commercial regains, ASTM D1909, is paywalled. The store page lists D1909-13(2026) as the active version. Its values were not read. [ASTM D1909 store page](https://store.astm.org/d1909-13r20.html)

**Moisture at 50 % RH and at saturation (resin datasheets)**
- At 23 °C / 50 % RH equilibrium, nylon 6,6 resin holds 2.6 % (ISO 62, 2 mm plaque). [Zytel 101L datasheet](https://Protolabs.com/media/tcebrq5j/zytel-101l.pdf)
- At 23 °C / 50 % RH, nylon 6 resin holds 2.7–3.2 % ("similar to ISO 62"). [BASF Ultramid B3S HP R01](https://download.basf.com/p1/8a80825f8ec1f32b018ee0f5a5bf6e14/en/Ultramid%25C2%25AE_B3S_HP_R01_UNCOLORED)
- Saturated by immersion in water at 23 °C, nylon 6,6 resin absorbs 8.5 % (ISO 62). [Zytel 101L datasheet](https://Protolabs.com/media/tcebrq5j/zytel-101l.pdf)
- Saturated in water at 23 °C, nylon 6 resin absorbs 9–10 % ("similar to ISO 62"). [BASF Ultramid B3S HP R01](https://download.basf.com/p1/8a80825f8ec1f32b018ee0f5a5bf6e14/en/Ultramid%25C2%25AE_B3S_HP_R01_UNCOLORED)
- Goodfellow lists nylon 6,6 fibre water absorption as 8 % "equilibrium" and 8.5 % in total, with no conditions stated. [Goodfellow: Nylon 6,6 fibre](https://www.goodfellow.com/usa/nylon-66-fibre-group)
- Synthesis (mine): nylon holds about 4–4.5 % in normal air. Roughly 8.5–10 % is reached only when the polymer is soaked to saturation, with nylon 6 at the top of that range. The deep-dive's "up to 10 %" is therefore a full-saturation figure for nylon 6, not typical in-use uptake.

**Nylon 6 vs nylon 6,6: abrasion, dye uptake, cost**
- Fulgar, an Italian maker of both yarns, calls PA6.6 more compact and more crystalline, with "superior abrasion resistance". No test data is given. Article dated 23 Feb 2026. [Fulgar: PA6 and PA6.6 differences](https://www.fulgar.com/en/feature/300/polyamide-6-and-polyamide-66-differences-performance-and-selection-criteria-in-textiles)
- Fulgar says PA6 has greater dye affinity and is easier to dye. [Fulgar](https://www.fulgar.com/en/feature/300/polyamide-6-and-polyamide-66-differences-performance-and-selection-criteria-in-textiles)
- Fulgar says PA6 is valued for its lower cost, but gives no figures. No numeric cost comparison was found. [Fulgar](https://www.fulgar.com/en/feature/300/polyamide-6-and-polyamide-66-differences-performance-and-selection-criteria-in-textiles)
- Carryology's 2019 article, written with EVERGOODS co-founder Kevin Dee, says nylon 6,6 is 20 % more abrasion resistant than conventional nylon, with 15 % higher tear and tensile strength. No test method is given. [Carryology: How to choose the right backpack fabric (23 Apr 2019)](https://www.carryology.com/insights/insights-1/how-to-choose-the-right-backpack-fabric/)
- The same article says nylon 6,6 often streaks in lighter colours because it dyes unevenly. [Carryology 2019](https://www.carryology.com/insights/insights-1/how-to-choose-the-right-backpack-fabric/)
- Ascend, a nylon 6,6 producer, credits the polymer's high melting temperature, dimensional stability ("lower creep") and abrasion resistance for its use in tyre cord and airbags. This is a maker claim. [Ascend: Fiber](https://www.ascendmaterials.com/fiber/)

**UV and wet strength**
- CAMEO says sunlight degrades nylon. [CAMEO: Nylon fiber](https://cameo.mfa.org/wiki/Nylon_fiber)
- Goodfellow rates nylon 6,6 fibre's UV resistance as "Poor", with no method or exposure conditions. [Goodfellow](https://www.goodfellow.com/usa/nylon-66-fibre-group)
- Carryology 2019 says nylon can weaken with prolonged UV exposure or with saturation over long periods. [Carryology 2019](https://www.carryology.com/insights/insights-1/how-to-choose-the-right-backpack-fabric/)

#### A2. How bag nylon is made

**Melt spinning and drawing**
- In melt spinning, polymer pellets are melted in an extruder, pumped through a spinneret and solidify as they cool. Nylon and polyester are both made this way. [Wikipedia: Spinning (polymers)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinning_(polymers))
- The spun filaments are then drawn (stretched) to align their structure and strengthen them. [Wikipedia: Spinning (polymers)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinning_(polymers))

**High-tenacity yarn**
- Monsanto patent US 3,716,611 (filed 1970, issued 13 Feb 1973) describes melt-spun nylon 6,6 yarn cold-drawn about 4.5–6.0×. It reaches 8.5–9.8 g/denier as drawn, and the patent claims at least 10 g/denier. [Google Patents: US3716611A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3716611A)
- The same patent says tyre-reinforcing nylon 6,6 rose from about 7 to 9.5 g/denier over the preceding decade. [US3716611A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3716611A)
- Ascend's current products: airbag fibre at 9.0–9.5 gpd (420–840 den) and tyre cord at up to 10.5 gpd. These are maker figures. [Ascend: Fiber](https://www.ascendmaterials.com/fiber/)
- Unirope lists nylon dry tenacity at 7.5–10.5 g/d. [Unirope TB21](https://www.unirope.com/wp-content/themes/unirope/pdfs/TB21_k-spec_fiber_characteristics.pdf)

**Air-jet textured (AJT) yarn**
- Cordura's Classic fabric page describes "100% high-tenacity, air jet-textured filament fiber" in 330D, 500D, 700D and 1000D. It is offered in plain, dobby, basket and ripstop weaves. [CORDURA Classic Fabric](https://cordura.com/classic-fabric)
- In air texturing, filaments are blasted with jets of air that roughen and loop them. [Carryology: History and science behind backpack textiles (23 Mar 2026)](https://www.carryology.com/liking/industry/the-fascinating-history-and-science-behind-backpack-textiles/)
- Air-jet textured yarns resemble spun (staple) yarns rather than stretch yarns, according to a Loughborough study of loop formation. [Loughborough repository: Loop formation mechanism in air-jet texturing](https://repository.lboro.ac.uk/articles/Loop_formation_mechanism_in_the_air-jet_texturing_process/9569375)
- DuPont's Taslan air-jet process dates from 1954. Heberlein's 1970 "Hemajet" three-hole nozzle made air texturing commercially practical and gave more bulk than false-twist texturing. [Fibre2Fashion: Air-jet texturising (A. K. Gandhi, Jan 2012)](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/industry-article/6057/air-jet-texturising-route-to-produce-spun)
- Sailrite's Cordura timeline puts the "first single-step nylon texturing machine" in Chattanooga, Tennessee, in the 1980s. [Sailrite: What is Cordura](https://www.sailrite.com/What-is-Cordura)

**Why some Cordura feels like cotton**
- Air texturing adds bulk and gives Cordura Classic a canvas-like hand. A Carryology reviewer called it "more cotton canvas like in look and feel". [Carryology: Ballistic nylon vs CORDURA (Janis Lacey, 9 Aug 2017)](https://www.carryology.com/insights/insights-1/ballistic-nylon-vs-cordura-heavy-duty-durable-fabrics/)
- Cordura Ballistic, by contrast, uses bright (lustrous) filament yarn rather than textured yarn, in a dense 2×2 basket weave with a "bright luster/sheen". [CORDURA Ballistic tech sheet (© 2022)](https://cordura.com/getmedia/469b3fb0-8e8a-4c16-9d52-f5d1f5fb698d/COR01549_CORDURA_Tech_Sheet_Ballistic_PRINT.pdf)

#### A3. Common bag nylons

**Packcloth**
- Rockywoods lists "lightweight nylon packcloth" at 210D and 420D, and "textured nylon or polyester packcloth" at 300D, 500D and 1000D, among Cordura-branded constructions. [Rockywoods: What is CORDURA fabric](https://rockywoods.com/pages/what-is-cordura%C2%AE-fabric-and-how-durable-is-it)
- A formal definition of "packcloth" was not found.

**210D and 420D ripstop**
- Ripstop has reinforcement yarns typically every 5–8 mm in a crosshatch. The same effect can come from weaving two or three fine yarns together. [Wikipedia: Ripstop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripstop)
- Carryology (2026) and ULA (2025) also give 5–8 mm spacing. [Carryology 2026](https://www.carryology.com/liking/industry/the-fascinating-history-and-science-behind-backpack-textiles/), [ULA: Ultra vs Robic (5 Mar 2025)](https://www.ula-equipment.com/2025/03/05/whats-the-difference-between-ultra-and-robic-fabrics)
- Carryology describes ripstop as a grid of doubled yarns, for example 420D rip yarns in a 210D fabric. [Carryology 2019](https://www.carryology.com/insights/insights-1/how-to-choose-the-right-backpack-fabric/)
- The same article notes that the raised grid is more exposed to abrasion and picking. [Carryology 2019](https://www.carryology.com/insights/insights-1/how-to-choose-the-right-backpack-fabric/)
- Counter-example to "5–8 mm": RBTR's 210D ROBIC uses a 3 mm × 3 mm box grid. [RBTR: 210D ROBIC Ripstop Nylon](https://ripstopbytheroll.com/products/210d-robic-ripstop-nylon)
- Not every 420D bag nylon is ripstop. EVERGOODS calls its 420D HT nylon 6,6 a "balanced woven textile". [EVERGOODS: Materials overview](https://evergoods.us/blogs/evergoods-road-to-launch/materials-overview)

**500D and 1000D Cordura:** see A4 and A7.

**1050D and 1680D ballistic**
- CORDURA Ballistic is 100 % high-tenacity nylon 6,6 bright filament yarn, above 420D, in at least a 2×2 basket weave. It can be coated or laminated. [CORDURA Ballistic tech sheet (© 2022)](https://cordura.com/getmedia/469b3fb0-8e8a-4c16-9d52-f5d1f5fb698d/COR01549_CORDURA_Tech_Sheet_Ballistic_PRINT.pdf)
- Cordura says the fabric is based on woven nylon originally developed for military body armour. [CORDURA Ballistic Fabric page](https://cordura.com/ballistic-fabric)
- Rockywoods' 1050D ballistic is a "traditional 2x2 basketweave" of high-tenacity yarn: 15.8 oz/yd², 0.033 in thick, PU back, DWR face, made in the USA. [Rockywoods: 1050D coated ballistic nylon](https://rockywoods.com/products/1050-denier-coated-ballistic-nylon-black-sold-per-yard)
- Rockywoods' 1680D ballistic nylon is "typically a 2x2 basket weave": 12.32 oz/yd², 0.021 in thick, PU back, DWR, non-USA. It is lighter than the 1050D product despite the higher denier. [Rockywoods: 1680D coated ballistic nylon](https://rockywoods.com/products/1680d-coated-ballistic-nylon-fabric-with-durable-water-repellent-finish)
- Today "ballistic nylon" is a loose term for basketweave nylon, typically 840D–1680D. [Carryology 2026](https://www.carryology.com/liking/industry/the-fascinating-history-and-science-behind-backpack-textiles/)
- The same article says ballistic is less abrasion-resistant than textured nylons despite its density. [Carryology 2026](https://www.carryology.com/liking/industry/the-fascinating-history-and-science-behind-backpack-textiles/)
- Carryology's 2017 comparison found ballistic heavier, with higher tensile and tear strength than Cordura Classic. Classic had greater abrasion resistance. [Carryology 2017](https://www.carryology.com/insights/insights-1/ballistic-nylon-vs-cordura-heavy-duty-durable-fabrics/)
- A strap and patch maker lists tear strength of 65–75 lb for 1000D textured plain weave and 95–110 lb for 1000D textured basket weave. Method not stated. [Milspecmonkey: Materials](https://milspecmonkey.com/index.php/customize/materials)

**Origin of ballistic nylon (claim: WWII flak-jacket fabric)**
- The US Army Medical Department history of WWII armour says nylon duck replaced cotton as the backing for Hadfield steel plates in flyer's armour by the end of 1943. A later vest backing is described as "ballistic nylon stock". [AMEDD Center of History & Heritage: Personnel Protective Armor (WWII wound ballistics, ch. XI)](https://achh.army.mil/history/book-wwii-woundblstcs-chapter11/)
- The same history says flyer's armour was aimed mainly at flak and low-velocity fragments, not pistol bullets. [AMEDD ch. XI](https://achh.army.mil/history/book-wwii-woundblstcs-chapter11/)
- The ground-troop M12 vest, with 8-ply 13 oz nylon duck, was standardised in August 1945. It was first used in the Korean War. [AMEDD ch. XI](https://achh.army.mil/history/book-wwii-woundblstcs-chapter11/)
- Carryology says DuPont developed a 1050D high-tenacity 2×2 basketweave in 1944 for flak jackets protecting bomber crews from fragments. The original specification called for 18 oz fabric. [Carryology 2026](https://www.carryology.com/liking/industry/the-fascinating-history-and-science-behind-backpack-textiles/)
- Wikipedia gives the same DuPont, WWII-airmen, 18 oz, 1050D, 2×2 description. It cites the AMEDD chapter. [Wikipedia: Ballistic nylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_nylon)
- A conflicting 2017 Carryology piece dates the name to Vietnam-era anti-fragmentation jackets. [Carryology 2017](https://www.carryology.com/insights/insights-1/ballistic-nylon-vs-cordura-heavy-duty-durable-fabrics/)

**Origin of ripstop (claim: WWII parachutes)**
- Heddels says parachute makers are credited with the first-known ripstop nylon, but its exact beginnings are "a bit hazy". Article from Jan 2021, updated Apr 2026. [Heddels: No More Tears – Ripping Into Ripstop](https://www.heddels.com/?p=166473)
- WWII diverted nylon to military uses, including parachute cloth. The article gives no origin date for ripstop itself. [Wikipedia: Ripstop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripstop)
- The earliest primary document I found is US patent 3,032,072, filed 26 Apr 1960 and issued 1 May 1962 by Army researchers. It refers to "conventional rip-stop threads" in standard parachute fabric, so ripstop parachute cloth was established by 1960. [Google Patents: US3032072A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3032072A/en)
- The US spec MIL-C-7020, "Cloth, Parachute, Nylon – Rip Stop and Twill Weave", lists revisions back to 8 Oct 1965. The current revision is 16 Mar 1992. [GlobalSpec: MIL-C-7020](https://standards.globalspec.com/std/510507/mil-c-7020)
- No WWII-dated primary source for ripstop was found.

#### A4. Cordura

**History: 1929 or 1967?**
- A rubber company asked DuPont in 1928 for a rayon tyre-cord yarn stronger than cotton. DuPont trademarked "Cordura" in 1929 and moved the brand to nylon in 1966. [Wikipedia: Cordura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordura)
- Carryology (23 Mar 2026) gives the same sequence: 1928 request, 1929 trademark as a rayon product, WWII use in military vehicle tyres, 1966 move to nylon. [Carryology 2026](https://www.carryology.com/liking/industry/the-fascinating-history-and-science-behind-backpack-textiles/)
- Rockywoods also says Cordura was developed by DuPont in 1929, originally as a rayon, with nylon formulations from 1966. [Rockywoods: What is CORDURA](https://rockywoods.com/pages/what-is-cordura%C2%AE-fabric-and-how-durable-is-it)
- Cordura's own timeline starts at 1967: "CORDURA® brand nylon 6,6 is commercially launched". [CORDURA: About](https://cordura.com/about)
- The Cordura home page reads "Trusted for durability. Since 1967". [CORDURA home](https://www.cordura.com/)
- Synthesis (mine): the name dates to 1929 as a rayon tyre cord. 1966–67 is the start of the nylon fabric brand, and Cordura itself counts from 1967.
- A primary DuPont or USPTO record for the 1929 trademark was not opened. That date rests on the secondary sources above.
- By 1979, soft-sided Cordura luggage held about 40 % of the luggage market. [Wikipedia: Cordura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordura); [Carryology 2026](https://www.carryology.com/liking/industry/the-fascinating-history-and-science-behind-backpack-textiles/)

**Owner in 2026**
- The Cordura site footer reads "© 2026 INVISTA", and CORDURA is an INVISTA trademark. [CORDURA home](https://www.cordura.com/)
- INVISTA has been a Koch subsidiary since 2004. [INVISTA press release, 29 Jul 2025](https://www.invista.com/news/invista-advances-cordura-brand-and-nylon-business-to-meet-evolving-customer-needs)
- On 29 July 2025, INVISTA said that after reviewing strategic alternatives it would keep its nylon fibres business, including CORDURA. It planned more than US$500 million of investment over five years. [INVISTA press release, 29 Jul 2025](https://www.invista.com/news/invista-advances-cordura-brand-and-nylon-business-to-meet-evolving-customer-needs)
- Textile World (29 Sep 2025, sponsored content supplied by INVISTA) repeats that INVISTA decided to retain the CORDURA Advanced Fabrics brand. [Textile World, Sep 2025](https://www.textileworld.com/textile-world/nonwovens-technical-textiles/2025/09/cordura-advanced-fabrics-proud-legacy-bold-future-open-for-business-and-committed-to-growth/)
- An Inside Denim headline, "A deal may be close as Invista seeks sale of Cordura", exists, but the article is behind a login and was not read. No completed sale was found. [Inside Denim (login wall)](https://insidedenim.com/News/170851)

**A brand and certification programme, not one fabric**
- Cordura's FAQ calls CORDURA an ingredient brand: it supplies engineered fibres to certified mill partners, and the mills make the fabrics. [CORDURA FAQ](https://cordura.com/FAQ)
- Only fabrics that meet Cordura's Brand Quality Standards earn certification and the hangtag. Cordura says the hangtag is trusted by more than 500 brands. [CORDURA: About](https://cordura.com/about)
- Cordura calls this a "partnership program" and does not use the word "licensing". [CORDURA: About](https://cordura.com/about)
- Hangtags and labels are optional. A product without one may still use CORDURA fabric. [CORDURA FAQ](https://cordura.com/FAQ)
- The name can apply to any textile made with INVISTA yarn, so "1000D Cordura" narrows the field without identifying one fabric. [Carryology 2019](https://www.carryology.com/insights/insights-1/how-to-choose-the-right-backpack-fabric/)
- Fabric spec sheets sit behind a MyCORDURA account ("Request Access") and were not accessed. [CORDURA Classic Fabric](https://cordura.com/classic-fabric)

**Fabric lines**

- Ballistic: high-tenacity nylon 6,6 bright filament, at least 2×2 basket weave (see A3). [CORDURA Ballistic tech sheet](https://cordura.com/getmedia/469b3fb0-8e8a-4c16-9d52-f5d1f5fb698d/COR01549_CORDURA_Tech_Sheet_Ballistic_PRINT.pdf)

- re/cor recycled range: RN66 from reclaimed post-industrial waste (polymer not stated), RN6 from 100 % recycled nylon 6 yarn, and RPET from recycled polyester. [CORDURA re/cor](https://cordura.com/re-cor-fabric)
- Newer additions: Comfort FR (2025) and the Adventurer collection (2026). [CORDURA: About](https://cordura.com/about)
- Polyester Cordura fabrics exist: CORDURA HP and re/cor RPET (details in section B2). [Rockywoods: 1000D CORDURA HP polyester](https://rockywoods.com/products/1000-denier-coated-cordura-hp-polyester-fabric); [CORDURA re/cor RPET tech sheet](https://cordura.com/getmedia/6b63e74a-7d46-46a0-8a32-2a55e6c4cc73/COR01657_CORDURA_Tech_Sheet_recor-E2-84-A2-_RPET_PRINT.pdf)

**Typical weights: 1000D Cordura**

#### A7. The worked weight example

**Arithmetic check (mine)**
- 500D means 500 g per 9,000 m, so 0.0556 g per metre. The post's 0.056 is correctly rounded.
- At 12 yarns/cm each way, there are 2 × 1,200 = 2,400 m of yarn per m². At 0.0556 g/m that is 133 g/m². The post's 134 comes from the rounded 0.056.
- Adding 10 % for crimp gives 147 g/m².
- Adding the post's own 30–50 g/m² of PU gives **177–197 g/m², not about 230.** The steps as written do not reach the stated total.
- No source was found for the "about 10 %" crimp figure, so it is unverified.

**Published construction data for 500D (US Army GL/PD 10-07, 2009)**
- Type III 500D textured nylon duck needs at least 48 warp × 35 fill yarns per inch, tested to ASTM D3775. That is 18.9 × 13.8 yarns/cm (conv.), well above 12 × 12. [GL/PD 10-07](https://ciehub.info/spec/PD/GL-PD-10-07_2009-10-26.pdf)
- Recalculated at those minimum counts: 3,268 m/m² × 0.0556 g/m gives 182 g/m², and +10 % crimp gives about 200 g/m² (5.9 oz/yd²). That sits just under the uncoated classes' 6.0–7.0 oz/yd² (203–237 g/m², conv.).
- The coated Class 3 range of 7.0–8.0 oz/yd² (237–271 g/m²) sits about 1 oz/yd² (34 g/m²) above the uncoated classes, matching a clear PU back coat. [GL/PD 10-07](https://ciehub.info/spec/PD/GL-PD-10-07_2009-10-26.pdf)
- So "about 230 g/m²" is a fair figure for finished coated 500D, but only with about 19 × 14 yarns/cm, not 12 × 12.

**Same check for 1000D**
- Ennis Cordura 1000 has 34 × 26 threads per inch (13.4 × 10.2 per cm). [Ennis Fabrics: Cordura 1000](https://ennisfabrics.com/products/cordura-1000)
- That gives 2,362 m/m² × 0.111 g/m = 262 g/m², or 289 g/m² with +10 % crimp. The published 329 g/m² leaves about 40 g/m² for coating and finish (my arithmetic).

**Published PU coating add-ons**
- Rockywoods 500D CORDURA: "approximately 0.75–1.0 oz/sq yd" of clear urethane, about 25–34 g/m² (conv.). [Rockywoods 500D coated CORDURA](https://rockywoods.com/products/500-denier-coated-cordura-nylon-fabric)
- Rockywoods 1000D CORDURA HP (polyester): "1 oz." PU back coating, about 34 g/m² (conv.). [Rockywoods 1000D CORDURA HP](https://rockywoods.com/products/1000-denier-coated-cordura-hp-polyester-fabric)
- Shelby Outdoor 500D Cordura PU (240 g/m²) gives its composition as 96 % polyamide and 4 % PU, which implies about 10 g/m² of PU (my arithmetic). [Shelby Outdoor: Cordura 500D PU coated](https://shelbyoutdoor.com/de/p103045/cordura-500d-with-dwr-pu-coated)
- Published add-ons for these fabrics therefore run about 10–34 g/m². The post's 30–50 g/m² is at or above the top of that range.
- No source was found giving the weight of a DWR finish.

### B. POLYESTER

#### B1. Chemistry and properties

**PET and the ester bond**
- The US FTC defines polyester fibre as a long-chain polymer that is at least 85 % by weight an ester of a dihydric alcohol and terephthalic acid. PET, polyethylene terephthalate, is the common example. [CAMEO: Polyester fiber](https://cameo.mfa.org/wiki/Polyester_fiber)
- PET is spun into fibre and also blow-moulded into drink bottles and extruded into film. [Britannica: Polyethylene terephthalate](https://www.britannica.com/science/polyethylene-terephthalate)
- The first viable polyester fibre, Terylene, was developed in 1941 by J. R. Whinfield and J. T. Dickson. It was first sold in the US as Dacron in 1951. [CAMEO: Polyester fiber](https://cameo.mfa.org/wiki/Polyester_fiber)
- Polyester, like nylon, is melt spun. [Wikipedia: Spinning (polymers)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinning_(polymers))

**Moisture regain**
- Polyester fibre regain: 0.4–0.8 % (Kadolph 1998 table). [Wikipedia: Yarn conditioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarn_conditioning)
- CAMEO gives 0.1–0.4 %. [CAMEO: Polyester fiber](https://cameo.mfa.org/wiki/Polyester_fiber)
- Unirope gives 1 %. [Unirope TB21](https://www.unirope.com/wp-content/themes/unirope/pdfs/TB21_k-spec_fiber_characteristics.pdf)
- Carryology calls polyester hydrophobic, saying it does not absorb water. [Carryology 2019](https://www.carryology.com/insights/insights-1/how-to-choose-the-right-backpack-fabric/)

**Density**
- Polyester specific gravity: 1.38, against 1.14 for nylon. [Unirope TB21](https://www.unirope.com/wp-content/themes/unirope/pdfs/TB21_k-spec_fiber_characteristics.pdf)
- CAMEO gives 1.23–1.38 g/ml across polyesters. [CAMEO: Polyester fiber](https://cameo.mfa.org/wiki/Polyester_fiber)
- Consequence (my arithmetic): at equal denier, a PET yarn has about 17 % less cross-section than nylon (1.14/1.38), and about 9 % smaller diameter.

**Melting point**
- 490 °F (254 °C) (Unirope). [Unirope TB21](https://www.unirope.com/wp-content/themes/unirope/pdfs/TB21_k-spec_fiber_characteristics.pdf)
- 238–290 °C across polyesters (CAMEO). [CAMEO: Polyester fiber](https://cameo.mfa.org/wiki/Polyester_fiber)

**Stretch and dimensional stability**
- Elongation at break: polyester 12–18 % against nylon 15–28 % (Unirope). Polyester stretches less, but "doesn't stretch" overstates it. [Unirope TB21](https://www.unirope.com/wp-content/themes/unirope/pdfs/TB21_k-spec_fiber_characteristics.pdf)
- CAMEO gives a polyester elongation range of 10–50 %. [CAMEO: Polyester fiber](https://cameo.mfa.org/wiki/Polyester_fiber)
- By the mid-20th century, woven polyester (Dacron) had replaced natural fibres as standard sailcloth because it holds its shape better and stretches less. [Carryology 2026](https://www.carryology.com/liking/industry/the-fascinating-history-and-science-behind-backpack-textiles/)

**UV resistance**
- Carryology says polyester has built-in UV resistance, while nylon weakens under prolonged UV. [Carryology 2019](https://www.carryology.com/insights/insights-1/how-to-choose-the-right-backpack-fabric/)
- CAMEO notes polyester still "degrades in direct sunlight". [CAMEO: Polyester fiber](https://cameo.mfa.org/wiki/Polyester_fiber)
- No quantitative side-by-side UV test (for example ISO 105-B02 or AATCC 16) was found.

**Static**
- CAMEO says polyester fibre builds up static charge. [CAMEO: Polyester fiber](https://cameo.mfa.org/wiki/Polyester_fiber)
- No source was found linking this to lint pickup in bags.

**Abrasion and tear compared with nylon**
- Ascend, a nylon 6,6 maker, says typical nylon 6,6 fabric lasts about 40,000 rubs against about 20,000 for polyester. No method is given; this is a maker claim from an interested party. [Ascend blog: Nylon 6,6, a better, more durable material (© 2024)](https://blog.ascendmaterials.com/nylon-66-a-better-more-durable-material)
- Ascend also says breaking nylon takes about 15 % more force than polyester at the same denier. Method not stated. [Ascend blog](https://blog.ascendmaterials.com/nylon-66-a-better-more-durable-material)
- Carryology calls polyester "less expensive and also generally less durable". [Carryology 2019](https://www.carryology.com/insights/insights-1/how-to-choose-the-right-backpack-fabric/)
- Unirope gives dry tenacity of 7.0–10.0 g/d for polyester and 7.5–10.5 g/d for nylon. [Unirope TB21](https://www.unirope.com/wp-content/themes/unirope/pdfs/TB21_k-spec_fiber_characteristics.pdf)
- No independent like-for-like woven bag-fabric test (same denier, weave and coating) was found.

### C. BOTH: coatings and finishes

**PU coating: hydrolysis, stickiness and flaking**
- A US Army Research Laboratory study of PU-coated fabrics found polyester-based (ester-type) urethanes more vulnerable to hydrolysis than polyether urethanes. [ARL-TR-6949: Hydrolytic stability of PU-coated fabrics (J. M. Sloan, Jun 2014)](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA606474.pdf)
- In that study, under water immersion at 180 °F (82 °C), one commercial PU-coated fabric fell apart after 42 days and two others after 70 days. Other products kept about 75–95 % of their seam strength. [ARL-TR-6949](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA606474.pdf)
- A long-standing outdoor-fabric FAQ explains that PU is hydrophilic, so water slowly attacks it. A PU tent packed wet for a long time can stick to itself. [Bushwalk FAQ: Silicone vs PU vs acrylic proofing (R. Caffin)](https://faq.bushwalk.com/FAQ_SiliconeVsPU.htm)
- The same FAQ says PU coatings can peel from nylon after years of hard use because they do not bond well to the fibres. [Bushwalk FAQ](https://faq.bushwalk.com/FAQ_SiliconeVsPU.htm)
- SylvanSport, a maker, says sticky or flaking fabric means the urethane coating is breaking down, and that this depends on age, care and storage. [SylvanSport support (© 2026)](https://support.sylvansport.com/when-your-tent-becomes-sticky-or-the-coating-flakes-off-the-urethane-coating-is-starting-to-break-down)
- SylvanSport's fix is to strip the old coat with rubbing alcohol and recoat. [SylvanSport support](https://support.sylvansport.com/when-your-tent-becomes-sticky-or-the-coating-flakes-off-the-urethane-coating-is-starting-to-break-down)
- A community repair guide names age, damp storage and insufficient off-gassing as accelerants. [iFixit: Tent Fly Sticks](https://www.ifixit.com/Wiki/Tent_Fly_Sticks)
- RBTR markets its "UTS" backside coating as an alternative to traditional PU with excellent hydrolysis performance. This is a seller claim. [RBTR: 210D ROBIC](https://ripstopbytheroll.com/products/210d-robic-ripstop-nylon)

**TPU coating**
- Extremtextil describes its TPU-ether film coating as hydrolysis-resistant, suitable for permanently damp uses. It can be welded coating-to-coating with an iron, hot air or RF at relatively low temperatures. [Extremtextil: Nylon 210den TPU-coated](https://www.extremtextil.de/en/nylon-210den-tpu-coated-one-side-heat-sealable-310g-sqm/72710)

**Silicone (sil-) coatings**
- Silicone soaks into the fabric rather than sitting on top. The Bushwalk FAQ reports silnylon can be up to 2.2× the tear strength of its base fabric, whereas PU can concentrate stress and weaken tear. [Bushwalk FAQ](https://faq.bushwalk.com/FAQ_SiliconeVsPU.htm)
- Silicone is hydrophobic, so it does not go sticky. [Bushwalk FAQ](https://faq.bushwalk.com/FAQ_SiliconeVsPU.htm)

**DWR and the move to PFAS-free (C0)**
- Mammut says long-chain C8 fluorinated DWRs were the industry standard for their durability. C6 was a bridge with faster-degrading by-products. Mammut dropped C8 in 2015 and began stepping back from C6 in 2016. [Mammut: The DWR dilemma (03/2023)](https://www.mammut.com/uk/en/stories-guides/waterproofing)
- Mammut says PFC-free DWR resists washing and wear less well, and needs regular washing and re-proofing. [Mammut](https://www.mammut.com/uk/en/stories-guides/waterproofing)
- Bag fabrics are moving to C0 now: ULA's 400 Robic has a "C0 PFAS-free DWR", and Extremtextil's Robic 70den carries an "FC-free C0 Impregnation". [ULA fabrics](https://www.ula-equipment.com/fabrics/); [Extremtextil Robic 70den](https://www.extremtextil.de/en/diamond-ripstop-nylon-robic-ocean-recycling-70den-uts-coated-100g-sqm/73078)

**PFAS rules: California (AB 1817)**
- AB 1817 was signed on 30 Sep 2022. [SGS SafeGuardS 120/22](https://www.sgs.com/en/news/2022/10/safeguards-12022-california-introduces-law-ab-1817-banning-pfas-on-apparel-and-textile-products)
- Its definition of textile articles explicitly includes handbags and backpacks. [SGS](https://www.sgs.com/en/news/2022/10/safeguards-12022-california-introduces-law-ab-1817-banning-pfas-on-apparel-and-textile-products)
- From 1 Jan 2025 it bans regulated PFAS at 100 ppm total organic fluorine or more. [SGS](https://www.sgs.com/en/news/2022/10/safeguards-12022-california-introduces-law-ab-1817-banning-pfas-on-apparel-and-textile-products)
- The threshold drops to 50 ppm on 1 Jan 2027. [Beveridge & Diamond (7 Oct 2026)](https://www.bdlaw.com/publications/new-eu-pfhxa-limits-take-effect-amid-expanding-pfas-rules-for-apparel-and-textiles/)
- Outdoor apparel for severe wet conditions has an extension to 2028, with a "Made with PFAS chemicals" disclosure from 2025. [SGS](https://www.sgs.com/en/news/2022/10/safeguards-12022-california-introduces-law-ab-1817-banning-pfas-on-apparel-and-textile-products)
- The bill text on leginfo.ca.gov did not load (see Unreachable).

**PFAS rules: New York**
- NY's apparel PFAS ban applies from 1 Jan 2025. [NY DEC: PFAS in apparel law (c. Sep 2025)](https://dec.ny.gov/environmental-protection/help-for-businesses/pfas-in-apparel-law)
- Outdoor apparel for severe wet conditions follows from 1 Jan 2028. [NY DEC](https://dec.ny.gov/environmental-protection/help-for-businesses/pfas-in-apparel-law)
- The DEC page lists clothing examples and does not mention bags. [NY DEC](https://dec.ny.gov/environmental-protection/help-for-businesses/pfas-in-apparel-law)
- DEC is drafting a concentration-based rule that must take effect by 1 Jan 2027. [Beveridge & Diamond](https://www.bdlaw.com/publications/new-eu-pfhxa-limits-take-effect-amid-expanding-pfas-rules-for-apparel-and-textiles/)

**PFAS rules: other US states** (all from [Beveridge & Diamond, 7 Oct 2026](https://www.bdlaw.com/publications/new-eu-pfhxa-limits-take-effect-amid-expanding-pfas-rules-for-apparel-and-textiles/))
- Maine: textiles with intentionally added PFAS banned from 1 Jan 2026. Outdoor apparel follows in 2029 unless it carries a disclosure.
- Colorado: textile articles and severe-wet outdoor apparel restricted from 1 Jan 2028.
- Washington: apparel and accessories restricted from 1 Jan 2027, with total fluorine above 50 ppm presumed to mean intentional addition.

**PFAS rules: EU**
- The EU's PFHxA restriction is Regulation (EU) 2024/2462, adding an entry to REACH Annex XVII. [Beveridge & Diamond](https://www.bdlaw.com/publications/new-eu-pfhxa-limits-take-effect-amid-expanding-pfas-rules-for-apparel-and-textiles/)
- It applies from 10 Oct 2026 to consumer clothing, accessories and footwear, and from 10 Oct 2027 to other textiles. [Beveridge & Diamond](https://www.bdlaw.com/publications/new-eu-pfhxa-limits-take-effect-amid-expanding-pfas-rules-for-apparel-and-textiles/)
- Its limits are 25 ppb for PFHxA and its salts, and 1,000 ppb for related substances. [Beveridge & Diamond](https://www.bdlaw.com/publications/new-eu-pfhxa-limits-take-effect-amid-expanding-pfas-rules-for-apparel-and-textiles/)
- Whether bags count as "accessories" was not confirmed.

## In use

### Nylon
- **Hand, AJT Cordura** (reviewer's observation): canvas-like, with a cotton-canvas look and feel. A slight pile collects dust and lint, and it reads as casual. [Carryology 2017](https://www.carryology.com/insights/insights-1/ballistic-nylon-vs-cordura-heavy-duty-durable-fabrics/)
- **Hand, ballistic** (reviewer's observation): smooth, glossy and polished. [Carryology 2017](https://www.carryology.com/insights/insights-1/ballistic-nylon-vs-cordura-heavy-duty-durable-fabrics/)
- **Hand, PU-coated nylon** (reviewer's observation): a PU back coat stiffens the fabric slightly and gives a crisp feel. [Carryology 2026](https://www.carryology.com/liking/industry/the-fascinating-history-and-science-behind-backpack-textiles/)
- **Sound and crinkle:** no source found.
- **How it takes abrasion** (reviewer's observation): AJT Classic outlasts ballistic in abrasion; ballistic wins on tear and tensile. [Carryology 2017](https://www.carryology.com/insights/insights-1/ballistic-nylon-vs-cordura-heavy-duty-durable-fabrics/)
- **How ripstop takes abrasion** (reviewer, with maker input): the raised ripstop grid takes abrasion and picks first. [Carryology 2019](https://www.carryology.com/insights/insights-1/how-to-choose-the-right-backpack-fabric/)
- **Scuffing and fuzzing:** no source found for how AJT or filament nylon fuzzes with wear.
- **Dyeing and looks** (reviewer, with maker input): light-coloured nylon 6,6 can streak. [Carryology 2019](https://www.carryology.com/insights/insights-1/how-to-choose-the-right-backpack-fabric/)
- **Dyeing and looks** (retailer guidance): coarse 1050D basketweave may show dye streaks or coating irregularities. [Rockywoods 1050D](https://rockywoods.com/products/1050-denier-coated-ballistic-nylon-black-sold-per-yard)
- **Water uptake** (expert FAQ): a wet PU-coated fabric stays heavy because the uncoated face soaks up water and dries slowly. [Bushwalk FAQ](https://faq.bushwalk.com/FAQ_SiliconeVsPU.htm)
- **Wet strength** (reviewer, with maker input): nylon can weaken when saturated for long periods. [Carryology 2019](https://www.carryology.com/insights/insights-1/how-to-choose-the-right-backpack-fabric/)
- **Sag when wet:** no source found.
- **UV ageing** (reviewer): nylon weakens with prolonged UV. [Carryology 2019](https://www.carryology.com/insights/insights-1/how-to-choose-the-right-backpack-fabric/)
- **UV ageing** (maker guidance): ULA says Robic is less UV-resistant than its Ultra fabric. [ULA blog](https://www.ula-equipment.com/2025/03/05/whats-the-difference-between-ultra-and-robic-fabrics)
- **Heat limits:** melting points are about 220 °C (nylon 6) and 262 °C (nylon 6,6) (see A1). No maker guidance on iron or dryer temperatures for bag fabric was found.
- **Cleaning, Cordura** (maker guidance): follow the bag brand's care instructions, because they cover the whole product. [CORDURA FAQ](https://cordura.com/FAQ)
- **Cleaning, Cordura fabric** (retailer guidance): hand wash with mild soap in cold water. [Sailrite 500D](https://www.sailrite.com/Cordura-500D-MultiCam-Original-60-Fabric)
- **Cleaning, Red Oxx bags** (bag maker guidance): mild soap, cold water and a soft brush, then air dry. Avoid excessive heat and harsh chemicals. [Red Oxx (17 Sep 2025)](https://www.redoxx.com/blogs/news/what-is-cordura-brand-nylon-how-is-it-different-from-canvas)
- **Cutting** (expert FAQ): cut plain nylon with a hot knife so the edges fuse. [Bushwalk FAQ](https://faq.bushwalk.com/FAQ_SiliconeVsPU.htm)
- **Repair** (maker guidance): ULA says Robic packs are easier and cheaper to repair than Ultra ones. [ULA blog](https://www.ula-equipment.com/2025/03/05/whats-the-difference-between-ultra-and-robic-fabrics)
- **Repair** (maker guidance): Tenacious Tape is sold as a fix for rips and tears in tents and backpacks. A separate silnylon version exists. [GEAR AID: Tenacious Tape](https://www.gearaid.com/products/tenacious-tape-repair-tape)
- **Repair** (maker guidance): GEAR AID says damage too big for Tenacious Tape should go to the bag maker or to Rainy Pass Repair. [GEAR AID FAQ](https://www.gearaid.com/pages/faqs)
- **Repair, darning and application steps:** no source found.
- **Longevity** (maker claim): ULA says "thousands of hikers" have done 10,000 miles on Robic packs. [ULA blog](https://www.ula-equipment.com/2025/03/05/whats-the-difference-between-ultra-and-robic-fabrics)
- **Weight vs Ultra** (maker staff comment, Oct 2026): ULA's 36L Dragonfly weighs 802 g in Robic and 879 g in Ultra 400TX. [ULA blog](https://www.ula-equipment.com/2025/03/05/whats-the-difference-between-ultra-and-robic-fabrics)

### Polyester
- **UV** (reviewer, with maker input): polyester has built-in UV resistance and suits gear left in the sun. [Carryology 2019](https://www.carryology.com/insights/insights-1/how-to-choose-the-right-backpack-fabric/)
- **UV** (materials database): polyester still degrades in direct sunlight. [CAMEO](https://cameo.mfa.org/wiki/Polyester_fiber)
- **Static** (materials database): polyester builds up static charge. [CAMEO](https://cameo.mfa.org/wiki/Polyester_fiber)
- **Wear** (reviewer, with maker input): 1680D polyester wears out faster than the nylon it imitates. [Carryology 2019](https://www.carryology.com/insights/insights-1/how-to-choose-the-right-backpack-fabric/)
- **Linings** (maker practice): ALPAKA lines Axoflux and X-Pac bags with polyester ripstops. [ALPAKA Elements Backpack Pro](https://alpakagear.com/en-de/products/elements-backpack-pro)
- **Weatherproofing** (maker claim, no test data): ALPAKA calls its Axoflux 300D wallet weatherproof and UV-proof. [ALPAKA Ark](https://alpakagear.com/products/ark-bifold-cash-wallet)
- **MYOG sources:** CORDURA HP polyester from Rockywoods and Sailrite. [Rockywoods HP](https://rockywoods.com/products/1000-denier-coated-cordura-hp-polyester-fabric); [Sailrite: What is Cordura](https://www.sailrite.com/What-is-Cordura)

### Shared: coating ageing and care
- **PU ageing** (owner reports, BackpackingLight forum, May 2024): a Black Diamond tent went sticky from PU hydrolysis; a PU pack cover became "a horrible sticky mess"; an Akto floor got sticky and smelly. Owners also said TPU and PEU coatings reportedly avoid the problem. [BPL forum thread](https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/why-do-tent-manufacturers-continue-to-use-pu-coatings/)
- **PU ageing** (lab study): ester-type PU hydrolyses faster than ether-type PU. [ARL-TR-6949](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA606474.pdf)
- **Reviving sticky PU** (maker guidance): soak, strip with rubbing alcohol, scrape, then recoat with Tent Sure, Revivex or Seam Grip. [SylvanSport](https://support.sylvansport.com/when-your-tent-becomes-sticky-or-the-coating-flakes-off-the-urethane-coating-is-starting-to-break-down)
- **Avoiding sticky PU** (community guide): dry gear before storage, let it off-gas, and use a breathable bag. [iFixit](https://www.ifixit.com/Wiki/Tent_Fly_Sticks)
- **DWR care** (maker guidance): PFC-free DWR needs regular washing and re-proofing because it is less resistant to wash and wear. [Mammut](https://www.mammut.com/uk/en/stories-guides/waterproofing)
- **TPU** (retailer guidance): TPU-ether coatings resist hydrolysis and can be heat-welded at home with an iron. [Extremtextil TPU](https://www.extremtextil.de/en/nylon-210den-tpu-coated-one-side-heat-sealable-310g-sqm/72710)
