# UHMWPE fabrics other than Challenge ULTRA: ALUULA Graflyte, Dyneema composites (DCF), woven UHMWPE for home makers, makers' house fabrics

Research for The Carry Index fabric pages. Collected 23 Oct 2026. These are facts with sources, not page copy.

Layout (per coordinator): **Part A: Graflyte** and **Part B: DCF / Dyneema composites** are separate top-level parts because each gets its own page. **Part C: UHMWPE overview** covers woven UHMWPE for home makers (MYOG), other UHMWPE fabrics bag makers use, and makers' house fabrics (ALPAKA Ultradyne and Axodyne). X-Pac's UHMWPE variants and Challenge ULTRA are left to the other agents.

Conventions:
- "my conversion" = arithmetic I did from the published figure (1 lbf = 4.448 N; 1 oz/yd² = 33.906 g/m²; 10,000 mm H₂O ≈ 0.981 bar ≈ 14.2 psi). It is not a published value.
- "maker claim" = the maker gives no test method or data.
- Partner brands (Durston, Nashville Pack, Arc'teryx, Db, Gossamer Gear, Hyperlite) are labelled as such; their fabric statements are second-hand maker claims.
- Web search budget for the session ran out late in the work. Gaps it caused are listed under Open questions.

## Findings

### PART A: ALUULA Graflyte

#### A1. What ALUULA itself says (construction, bonding, monomaterial)
- ALUULA's technology page describes its process as "fusing together mono-polymer fibers at the molecular level". It says the result has no adhesives and can be sewn, heat-welded or bonded to itself. Maker claim, no test data. [ALUULA, Technology](https://aluula.com/technology/)
- The technology page does not describe a film, a face fabric or a weave for any product. Those details come only from partner brands (see A2). [ALUULA, Technology](https://aluula.com/technology/)
- Monomaterial wording varies on ALUULA's own pages. The technology page makes a general claim that its materials are a single substance, 100% polyethylene, but elsewhere on the same page says only that most of its fabrics are 100% polyethylene. [ALUULA, Technology](https://aluula.com/technology/)
- ALUULA's homepage says its process removes the need for adhesives, giving materials that are 100% polyethylene and recycle-ready. [ALUULA home](https://aluula.com/)
- ALUULA's Outdoor Gear materials page is more cautious about delamination: "Our adhesive-free design minimizes the risk of delamination". The same page calls the fabrics heat-weldable, waterproof, UV-resistant and fully recyclable, with no figures. [ALUULA, Outdoor Gear materials](https://aluula.com/materials-outdoor-gear/)
- That page does not use the word "Graflyte" in its text; it appears only in image filenames. It lists products by number in three groups:
  - "Soft handfeel and unique look": ALUULA 52, 78, 97 and 125. Their image files are named V52, V78, V98 and Graflyte-Material.
  - "Extra durable reinforcements": ALUULA 115, 175, 155, 220, 24X and 46X (files HV115, HV175, HH155, HH220, AX24, AX46).
  - "Light as a feather": ALUULA 24 and 34 (files A24, A34).
  - No weights or test data are given. Note that ALUULA writes "97" where partners write "V-98". [ALUULA, Outdoor Gear materials](https://aluula.com/materials-outdoor-gear/)
- ALUULA's Outdoor page names Graflyte users: Db (Weigh Lighter), Nashville Pack and PAAGO WORKS (Rush 11R SP). It also names Durlyte for Rockgeist panniers, describing Durlyte as UHMWPE films fused to a UHMWPE woven core. [ALUULA, Outdoor](https://aluula.com/outdoor/)
- ALUULA's Care page has repair centres and repair guides for windsports products only (Aeris, Aeris X, Gold). It has no care or repair guidance for outdoor or Graflyte products. [ALUULA, Care](https://aluula.com/care/)

#### A2. Construction as described by partner brands
- Durston's description of Graflyte V-98:
  - a composite with a 100% UHMWPE woven face fabric and a "heat welded UHMWPE film that is delamination proof";
  - ALUULA's proprietary process fuses a waterproof film to the UHMWPE fibres without glue.
  - Partner and maker claim. [Durston, Wapta 30](https://durstongear.com/products/wapta-30)
- Nashville Pack's materials page lists Graflyte V98 as a 2-layer laminate: 100% UHMWPE face plus a PE film, 2.9 oz/yd². [Nashville Pack, Materials](https://nashvillepack.com/pages/materials)
- Nashville's Graflyte page says the film backing is chemically bonded straight to the face fabric by a proprietary fusion process, with no glue and no polyester blend. [Nashville Pack, ALUULA Graflyte](https://nashvillepack.com/pages/aluula-graflyte%E2%84%A2)
- Arc'teryx (Alpha SL 30 product page) lists two Graflyte variants, both labelled 100% polyethylene:
  - Body: 200d weave, "Graflyte VA", 130 gsm.
  - Contrast: 50d plain weave, "Graflyte V", 52 gsm.
  - The page says the fabric layers ultralight polymer films with a patented fusion process. Fabric origin is Canada; the pack is made in Vietnam. [Arc'teryx, Alpha SL 30](https://arcteryx.com/be/en/shop/alpha-sl-30-backpack-9660)
- Arc'teryx's launch post (9 May 2025) calls Graflyte a "three-layer construction". That conflicts with Nashville's two-layer description, and ALUULA publishes no layup. [Arc'teryx blog, Alpha SL launch](https://blog.arcteryx.com/news/arcteryx-launches-new-ultralight-ultra-durable-climbing-pack/)
- Db's Weigh Lighter page calls the fabric "A next-generation, ultra-lightweight monomaterial made from 100% polyethylene". It says the fabric is UV- and water-resistant and ready for recycling. No weights or tests. Undated page, ©2026. [Db, Weigh Lighter](https://dbjourney.com/en-gb/pages/weigh-lighter)
- A retailer's copy for the Db Weigh Lighter Backpack 25L calls Graflyte an "HDPE mono-material" and describes it as translucent. Retailer wording; it disagrees with Durston's description of a UHMWPE film. [Altitude Sports, Db Weigh Lighter 25L](https://www.altitude-sports.com/p/db-journey-weigh-lighter-backpack-25l-dbb-1000431400701)
- In Garage Grown Gear's article (18 Dec 2024), ALUULA's Dave Westwood says most fabrics moved to a 100% PE monopolymer about two years earlier. Nashville's Grant Leonardi describes molecular-level fusion of mono-polymer fibres. [Garage Grown Gear, Graflyte article](https://garagegrowngear.com/blogs/gear-gab/aluula-graflyte-weaving-a-new-future-for-ultralight-fabric)
- The launch press release (31 Jan 2024) says Graflyte has no added polyester film and can be heat-bonded to itself instead of sewn. It names Graflyte V-98 for the Durston Wapta 30. [ALUULA press release via Shop-Eat-Surf](https://shop-eat-surf-outdoor.com/press-releases/aluula-partners-with-arcteryx-and-durston/134649)
- **Monomaterial verdict:**
  - ALUULA's own pages say its materials are 100% polyethylene, while also saying only that most of its fabrics are.
  - Partners' spec labels (Arc'teryx, Gossamer Gear, Db) say 100% polyethylene for Graflyte specifically.
  - What the film is (UHMWPE, generic PE or HDPE) is described differently by different partners; ALUULA doesn't say.

  - Durlyte 115–220 gsm (film plus ALUULA Core);
  - Aeris 24–98 gsm.
- Arc'teryx lists "Graflyte VA" at 130 gsm (200d weave) and "Graflyte V" at 52 gsm (50d plain weave). 130 gsm is above Carryology's top figure of 125 gsm. [Arc'teryx, Alpha SL 30](https://arcteryx.com/be/en/shop/alpha-sl-30-backpack-9660)

[Field Mag, Mirage review](https://www.fieldmag.com/articles/gossamer-gear-alchemy-mirage-backpack-review)
- The Inertia (18 Jan 2024) gives Graflyte as 1.53–2.89 oz/yd² depending on version, which is 52–98 g/m² by my conversion. [The Inertia](https://www.theinertia.com/gear/game-changing-fabric-outdoors-market-recyclable/)
- Durlyte HH-190 is used, laser cut, for the Wapta 30's daisy chains. No weight is given for HH-190. [Durston, Wapta 30](https://durstongear.com/products/wapta-30)
- Aeris and Aeris X (windsports, July 2023): UHMWPE core weaves with outer films joined by a "patented fusion process". Aeris X adds an X weave for bias strength. Relative claims only (e.g. 6x the tear resistance of competing canopy fabrics), no methods. [Newsfile, ALUULA Aeris release](https://www.newsfilecorp.com/release/175024)

#### A4. Test data (tear, abrasion, waterproofness)
- **No numeric Graflyte test data with a named method was found from ALUULA or any partner.**
- The Inertia (Jan 2024) reports that ALUULA's "8x stronger than steel" figure comes from in-house testing and that no official test data had been released. [The Inertia](https://www.theinertia.com/gear/game-changing-fabric-outdoors-market-recyclable/)
- Durston's relative claims (maker claim, no figures or method):
  - Graflyte has higher tear strength and abrasion resistance than every comparable fabric.
  - ULTRA's UltraWeave is about 30% heavier than Graflyte, with about 30% less tear strength and abrasion resistance. [Durston, Wapta 30](https://durstongear.com/products/wapta-30)
- Durston says the Wapta 30 has not been tested to a waterproofness standard and isn't guaranteed waterproof. [Durston, Wapta 30](https://durstongear.com/products/wapta-30)
- Arc'teryx claims unmatched tear strength for the weight and eight times the strength-to-weight of steel. It lists the Alpha SL 30 as water resistant, with no figures. [Arc'teryx, Alpha SL 30](https://arcteryx.com/be/en/shop/alpha-sl-30-backpack-9660)
- Lost Ponies' listing claims 8–15x the strength-to-weight of steel and calls V-78 "waterproof, airtight" but not submersible. No method given. [GGG, Lost Ponies Zip Pouch](https://www.garagegrowngear.com/products/zip-pouch-by-lost-ponies)

### PART B: Dyneema composites (DCF, DCF Hybrid, Dyneema Woven Composite)

#### B1. Who makes it now
- dyneema.com carries Avient branding: Avient logo, Avon Lake, OH address and a "©2025 Avient Corporation" footer. [Dyneema home](https://www.dyneema.com/)
- Avient's current site (which shows a Q2 2026 earnings presentation) still links "Protective Materials" to dyneema.com. [Avient, News archive](https://www.avient.com/news/archives)
- No report of a sale, spin-off or strategic review of the Dyneema business was found in the pages opened. **That Avient still owns it in late Oct 2026 is the best-supported reading, but it is not fully confirmed** (search ran out before I could check 2026 news).
- Avient announced its purchase of DSM Protective Materials, including Dyneema, on 20 Apr 2022. Secondary source, citing Avient's announcement. [Wikipedia, DCF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyneema_Composite_Fabric)
- Extremtextil (updated 23 Jul 2026) gives the ownership chain as Cuben Fiber (1990s), Cubic Tech, DSM (2015), then Avient (2022). [Extremtextil, Dyneema composites info](https://www.extremtextil.de/en/know-how/infos-on-dyneema-composites)
- DCF is made at Dyneema's production site in Mesa, Arizona, per Dyneema's own post of 30 Jun 2022. [Dyneema blog, Made in the USA](https://www.dyneema.com/about-us/latest-news/blog/dyneema-composite-fabric-proudly-made-in-the-usa) Extremtextil lists the country of origin as USA. [Extremtextil, DCF 0.96](https://www.extremtextil.de/en/dyneema-composite-fabric-0.96-33g-sqm/71711)

#### B3. Construction
- **Composite Fabric**: "Polyester film - 0°/90° Dyneema® fiber - Polyester film". There is no woven layer. [Dyneema, Composite Fabric 1.6](https://www.dyneema.com/fabric-finder/dyneema-composite-fabric-16/ct5k18-nat)
- Film thickness by grade: 0.08 mil on CT1E.08 and CT2E.08; 0.18 mil on the K.18 grades. Colour comes from coloured fibres, so black reads grey. [Extremtextil](https://www.extremtextil.de/en/know-how/infos-on-dyneema-composites)
- **Composite Hybrid**: a woven face on one side, the 0°/90° Dyneema fibre core, and polyester film on the other side. Face fabrics by grade:
  - 3.2 and 3.8: 50d woven polyester;
  - 5.0: 70d nylon;
  - 5.1 and 5.9: 150d woven polyester.
  - Sources: Dyneema product pages [3.2](https://www.dyneema.com/fabric-finder/dyneema-composite-hybrid-32/ct5k18wov32c), [3.8](https://www.dyneema.com/fabric-finder/dyneema-composite-hybrid-38/ct9hk18wov32c), [5.0](https://www.dyneema.com/fabric-finder/dyneema-composite-hybrid-50/ct9hk18bkc29), [5.1](https://www.dyneema.com/fabric-finder/dyneema-composite-hybrid-51/ct9hk18wov6), [5.9](https://www.dyneema.com/fabric-finder/dyneema-composite-hybrid-59/ct9hk18blkwov6)
- RBTR describes the "2.92 oz" hybrid as a 1.43 oz/yd² DCF plus a 50D polyester plain-weave face, and the "5.0 oz" hybrid as a 2.15 oz/yd² DCF plus a 150D polyester plain-weave face. [RBTR, 2.92 oz Hybrid](https://ripstopbytheroll.com/products/2-92-oz-dyneema-composite-fabric-hybrid-ct5k-18-wov-32c); [RBTR, 5.0 oz Hybrid](https://ripstopbytheroll.com/products/5-0-oz-dyneema-composite-fabric-hybrid-ct9hk-18-wov-6)
- **Dyneema Woven Composite** (launched with Hyperlite, available from 15 Jul 2025):
  - 100% woven Dyneema face bonded to a 0°/90° Dyneema core, with polyester film inside.
  - Grades: 3.1 (100d face, 107 gsm) and 3.9 (200d face, 132 gsm).
  - Dyneema labels both "Developmental fabric, properties may be subject to change".
  - [Avient release](https://www.avient.com/node/23532); [Dyneema, Woven Composite 3.9](https://www.dyneema.com/fabric-finder/dyneema-woven-composite-39/xctd200-wh01); [Dyneema, Woven Composite 3.1](https://www.dyneema.com/fabric-finder/dyneema-woven-composite-31/xctd100-wh01)
- Dyneema says the bonded face has almost no fibre crimp, and calls the Woven Composite its "most durable composite yet". [Dyneema, Woven Composites](https://www.dyneema.com/design-with-dyneema/dyneema-woven-composites); [Dyneema, Tents/Packs/Bags](https://www.dyneema.com/sectors/sports-and-consumer-products/tents-packs-and-bags)
- Dyneema's backpack brochure shows the Woven Composite layers as 100% woven Dyneema, then 0° and 90° Dyneema fibre, then film. [Dyneema backpack brochure (PDF)](https://www.dyneema.com/media/0bwhj3as/dyn252-pr-backpack-digital-brochure-aw-02.pdf)
- PlasticsToday (14 Jul 2025) reports Avient's relative claims for the Woven Composite: up to 10x abrasion resistance, 5x tear resistance and 34% weight saving versus its existing composites. Maker claim, no method. [PlasticsToday](https://www.plasticstoday.com/materials/dyneema-debuts-new-dual-layer-composite-fabric)
- All Dyneema composites have used bio-circular-feedstock fibre since 2020. [Dyneema, Tents/Packs/Bags](https://www.dyneema.com/sectors/sports-and-consumer-products/tents-packs-and-bags)

#### B4. Test data (all from Dyneema product pages; full numbers in the Data table)
- Dyneema uses the same methods for every grade:
  - tensile: ASTM D3039, lb/in;
  - tear: "Slit method Mil-C-21189 10.2.4", lb, a single value;
  - puncture: ASTM F1342 Probe B, lb;
  - water resistance: AATCC TM127, mm H₂O.
  - [Dyneema, Hybrid 3.2](https://www.dyneema.com/fabric-finder/dyneema-composite-hybrid-32/ct5k18wov32c)
- **No abrasion figures are published for any grade.** Dyneema says it runs Taber abrasion (and lists Martindale on another page) but gives no results. [Fabric Finder Guide](https://www.dyneema.com/design-with-dyneema/fabric-finder-guide); [Dyneema, Tents/Packs/Bags](https://www.dyneema.com/sectors/sports-and-consumer-products/tents-packs-and-bags)
- Water resistance by grade: 0.55 and 0.66 are 15,000 mm H₂O; 0.75 and 0.96 are 20,000 mm; every heavier grade is ">20,000 mm". [Dyneema, 0.55](https://www.dyneema.com/fabric-finder/dyneema-composite-fabric-055/ct1e08-nat); [0.66](https://www.dyneema.com/fabric-finder/dyneema-composite-fabric-066/ct1e08k18-nat)
- Woven Composite 3.9 tear is 250 lb (my conversion ≈1,112 N), about six times Hybrid 3.2 (42 lb) on the same method. [Dyneema, Woven Composite 3.9](https://www.dyneema.com/fabric-finder/dyneema-woven-composite-39/xctd200-wh01)
- HMG's relative claims (no absolute abrasion numbers): Woven Composite 3.9 has 10x the abrasion resistance of Hybrid 3.8 at similar weight, and 2x that of Hybrid 5.1 while 25% lighter. Maker claim. [HMG, Deep dive](https://hyperlitemountaingear.com/blogs/the-trailhead/a-deep-dive-into-our-new-pack-fabric)
- **Possible data error:** the 3.8 and 5.1 pages both show 43 lb tear and 9.1 lb puncture, although their face fabrics and weights differ. [Dyneema, Hybrid 3.8](https://www.dyneema.com/fabric-finder/dyneema-composite-hybrid-38/ct9hk18wov32c); [Hybrid 5.1](https://www.dyneema.com/fabric-finder/dyneema-composite-hybrid-51/ct9hk18wov6)

#### B5. Where DCF fits: ultralight packs, abrasion and puncture
- Extremtextil: plain Composite Fabric suits ultralight use without heavy abrasion. Rough surfaces and sharp edges damage it, and once damaged it "is no longer waterproof". Hybrids add a woven face for abrasion resistance. Retailer guidance. [Extremtextil](https://www.extremtextil.de/en/know-how/infos-on-dyneema-composites)
- Extremtextil's listing for plain 0.96 calls it "prone to abrasion" and says to handle it with care. [Extremtextil, DCF 0.96](https://www.extremtextil.de/en/dyneema-composite-fabric-0.96-33g-sqm/71711)
- Puncture resistance rises with weight: 1.8 lb (0.55) up to 11.4 lb (Woven Composite 3.9), all ASTM F1342 Probe B. [Dyneema, 0.55](https://www.dyneema.com/fabric-finder/dyneema-composite-fabric-055/ct1e08-nat); [Woven Composite 3.9](https://www.dyneema.com/fabric-finder/dyneema-woven-composite-39/xctd200-wh01)
- SectionHiker (Jan 2023, updated May 2025): hybrids are laminated with polyester to add abrasion resistance. Sewing straps and pockets onto DCF makes holes that leak unless sealed, so most DCF pack makers no longer call their packs watertight. Reviewer's observation. [SectionHiker, DCF FAQ](https://sectionhiker.com/dyneema-composite-fabrics-faq/)
- Garage Grown Gear (Jan 2023) ran an informal nine-month side-by-side of two Zpacks Nero packs, one in DCF Hybrid (50D polyester face) and one in ULTRA 100/200 (no lab method):
  - both wore at the bottom corners, the ULTRA slightly slower;
  - neither tore or delaminated.
  - Reviewer's observation. [GGG, DCF vs Ultra](https://www.garagegrowngear.com/en-au/blogs/trail-talk/dcf-vs-ultra-fabric-test-ultralight-packs)
- HMG's current core packs (Southwest, Junction, Windrider) use "Dyneema® Woven Composite 3.9, Hardline". Southwest 40 and Junction 40 are $395. Junction 40 (size M) is 832.7 g white or 847.6 g black. [HMG, Junction 40](https://www.hyperlitemountaingear.com/products/junction-40); [HMG, Southwest 40](https://www.hyperlitemountaingear.com/products/southwest-40)
- HMG says these packs are seam-taped on the vertical side seams and behind sewn features and are nearly waterproof when used with stuff sacks, while the fabric itself is fully waterproof. Maker claim. [HMG, Southwest 40](https://www.hyperlitemountaingear.com/products/southwest-40)

## Deep-dive claims checked

### Part A: Graflyte
1. **"Graflyte (ALUULA Composites) is a newer UHMWPE-based fabric entering the ultralight pack market."** CONFIRMED. Durston Wapta 30 (2024), Gossamer Gear Mirage 40 (Dec 2025) and Arc'teryx Alpha SL 30 (May 2025). [Durston](https://durstongear.com/products/wapta-30); [Gear Patrol, Mirage](https://www.gearpatrol.com/outdoors/gossamer-gear-mirage-40/)
2. **"100% UHMWPE face fabric, unlike ULTRA (blend) or DCF Hybrids (polyester or nylon faces)."** CONFIRMED as partner claims. Durston and Nashville say the woven face is 100% UHMWPE; Arc'teryx and Gossamer Gear label it 100% polyethylene. [Durston](https://durstongear.com/products/wapta-30); [Nashville](https://nashvillepack.com/pages/aluula-graflyte%E2%84%A2)
   - **Caveat:** Durston's claim that Graflyte is the only composite with a 100% UHMWPE face is out of date. Dyneema Woven Composite (July 2025) also has a 100% woven Dyneema (UHMWPE) face. [Avient](https://www.avient.com/node/23532)
3. **"Two-layer construction: face fabric + film, no separate cross-ply."** PARTLY / UNCONFIRMED.
   - Nashville says two layers (UHMWPE face + PE film). [Nashville materials](https://nashvillepack.com/pages/materials)
   - Arc'teryx's launch post says "three-layer construction". [Arc'teryx blog](https://blog.arcteryx.com/news/arcteryx-launches-new-ultralight-ultra-durable-climbing-pack/)
   - ALUULA publishes no layup, so whether there's a cross-ply is unconfirmed.
4. **"Molecular fusion bonding, no adhesive; bonds UHMWPE face directly to a polyethylene film at the molecular level."** PARTLY.
   - ALUULA's own wording is "fusing together mono-polymer fibers at the molecular level", with no adhesives. [ALUULA, Technology](https://aluula.com/technology/)
   - The film is described only by partners: a waterproof UHMWPE film (Durston), a PE film (Nashville), and an HDPE mono-material (retailer copy for Db).
5. **"No glue, no delamination."** PARTLY.
   - ALUULA's Outdoor Gear page says the design reduces the risk of delamination, while its Technology page claims no risk. [ALUULA, Outdoor Gear](https://aluula.com/materials-outdoor-gear/)
   - Arc'teryx's care label warns delamination may occur after prolonged exposure to high temperature. [Arc'teryx](https://arcteryx.com/be/en/shop/alpha-sl-30-backpack-9660)
6. **"Graflyte V-98 weighs 98 g/m² vs ULTRA 100X at 112 g/m²."** CONFIRMED.
   - V-98 is 98 g/m². [Durston](https://durstongear.com/products/wapta-30)
   - ULTRA 100 X is 112 g/m² per Extremtextil's listing. [Extremtextil](https://www.extremtextil.de/en/challengeultra)
   - Lighter Graflyte grades (V52 at 52 gsm, V-78) and a heavier one (VA at 130 gsm) also exist.
7. **"Still only making its way into the ultralight world; a while before EDC and travel packs."** PARTLY (out of date).
   - By late 2026 there are the Db Weigh Lighter Sling 10L and Split Duffel 70L, Lost Ponies Graflyte pouches and zip cubes, and the Arc'teryx Alpha SL 30, which Gear Patrol recommends for EDC and travel. [Db](https://dbjourney.com/en-gb/pages/weigh-lighter); [GGG collection](https://www.garagegrowngear.com/collections/aluula-graflyte); [Gear Patrol](https://www.gearpatrol.com/?p=895565)
   - No EDC-first brand's Graflyte backpack was found (search was limited).

### Part B: DCF
8. **"Dyneema is DSM's brand name … DSM dominates."** WRONG (out of date). Avient has owned Dyneema since 2022, and dyneema.com carries a ©2025 Avient footer. [Dyneema home](https://www.dyneema.com/); [Extremtextil](https://www.extremtextil.de/en/know-how/infos-on-dyneema-composites)
9. **"DSM has been producing UHMWPE since the 1970s."** PARTLY. Dyneema's site says "In 1969, we invented the world's strongest fiber™". I did not check production start dates. [Dyneema home](https://www.dyneema.com/)
10. **"DCF: UHMWPE fibres aligned in a grid between two sheets of waterproof polyester film; no woven face."** CONFIRMED. Dyneema gives "Polyester film - 0°/90° Dyneema® fiber - Polyester film". [Dyneema, 1.6](https://www.dyneema.com/fabric-finder/dyneema-composite-fabric-16/ct5k18-nat)
11. **"DCF … 99 g/m²."** WRONG.
    - Plain Dyneema Composite Fabric runs 19–53 gsm (0.55–1.6 oz/yd²). [Dyneema Fabric Finder](https://www.dyneema.com/fabric-finder)
    - 99 gsm matches RBTR's listing of the *Hybrid* CT5K.18/wov.32c ("2.92 oz"). Dyneema itself lists that SKU at 108 gsm. [RBTR](https://ripstopbytheroll.com/products/2-92-oz-dyneema-composite-fabric-hybrid-ct5k-18-wov-32c)
12. **"Hybrid replaces the outer film with a woven fabric, usually 50D polyester or nylon."** PARTLY.
    - Faces are 50d polyester (3.2, 3.8), 70d nylon (5.0 only) and 150d polyester (5.1, 5.9).
    - The other side keeps a polyester film. [Dyneema product pages](https://www.dyneema.com/fabric-finder)
13. **Table: "DCF Hybrid 3.2: 50D woven polyester, 108 g/m², ~187 N, 2+ bar."** CONFIRMED, with nuance.
    - 3.2 oz/yd² (108 gsm); tear 42 lb on the slit method Mil-C-21189 10.2.4 (≈187 N, my conversion); >20,000 mm H₂O on AATCC TM127.
    - 20,000 mm ≈ 1.96 bar, so "~2 bar" is fairer than "2+ bar".
    - The black version is 3.1 oz/yd² (104 gsm). [Dyneema, 3.2](https://www.dyneema.com/fabric-finder/dyneema-composite-hybrid-32/ct5k18wov32c)
14. **Table: "DCF Hybrid 5.0: 70D nylon, 170 g/m², ~271 N, 2+ bar."** CONFIRMED for Dyneema's Hybrid 5.0 (CT9HK.18/BKC29 and CC8): 170 gsm, 61 lb slit tear (≈271 N), >20,000 mm. [Dyneema, 5.0](https://www.dyneema.com/fabric-finder/dyneema-composite-hybrid-50/ct9hk18bkc29)
    - **Trap for readers:** the hybrid most often sold as "5.0 oz" (RBTR, CT9HK.18/wov.6) is a **150D polyester** face. Dyneema calls it Hybrid 5.1 (174 gsm, 43 lb tear). [RBTR 5.0](https://ripstopbytheroll.com/products/5-0-oz-dyneema-composite-fabric-hybrid-ct9hk-18-wov-6); [Dyneema 5.1](https://www.dyneema.com/fabric-finder/dyneema-composite-hybrid-51/ct9hk18wov6)
15. **"DCF Hybrid tear strength is reported as a single value."** CONFIRMED.
    - Dyneema uses a single slit-method value (Mil-C-21189 10.2.4).
    - It is a different method from the warp/fill tongue or Elmendorf figures in the posts' X-Pac and ULTRA rows, so putting them in one column without a note is misleading. [Dyneema, 3.2](https://www.dyneema.com/fabric-finder/dyneema-composite-hybrid-32/ct5k18wov32c)
16. **"Abrasion data isn't available for these variants."** CONFIRMED. Dyneema says it runs Taber abrasion but publishes no figures. [Fabric Finder Guide](https://www.dyneema.com/design-with-dyneema/fabric-finder-guide)
17. **"Given the thin woven face (50–70D), it's safe to assume significantly lower abrasion numbers than ULTRA or X-Pac."** UNCONFIRMED (an assumption).
    - No published figures exist.
    - The 5.1 and 5.9 grades have a 150D face.
    - The only data are relative maker claims (HMG: Woven Composite 3.9 = 10x Hybrid 3.8, 2x Hybrid 5.1). [HMG](https://hyperlitemountaingear.com/blogs/the-trailhead/a-deep-dive-into-our-new-pack-fabric)
18. **"2 bar means DCF is waterproof to over 20 m depth."** CONFIRMED as arithmetic for ">20,000 mm H₂O" (static water column). The lightest grades (0.55, 0.66) are 15,000 mm. [Dyneema, 0.55](https://www.dyneema.com/fabric-finder/dyneema-composite-fabric-055/ct1e08-nat)
19. **"DCF Hybrid 3.2 at 108 g/m² is the weight champion."** PARTLY (out of date). In the posts' table it is the lightest, but Dyneema Woven Composite 3.1 is 107 gsm and Graflyte V-98 is 98 g/m². [Dyneema, WC 3.1](https://www.dyneema.com/fabric-finder/dyneema-woven-composite-31/xctd100-wh01)
20. **"DCF makes perfect sense for ultralight hiking bags."** Opinion. Retailer guidance agrees for low-abrasion ultralight use. [Extremtextil](https://www.extremtextil.de/en/know-how/infos-on-dyneema-composites)
21. **Caption: "Hyperlite Mountain Gear Junction (40L backpack that weighs 820 grams)."** PARTLY.
    - The current Junction 40 (Woven Composite 3.9) lists 832.7 g (white) / 847.6 g (black) in size M.
    - Its header says 28.8 oz (≈816 g).
    - Whether 820 g matched the older DCH model is unconfirmed. [HMG, Junction 40](https://www.hyperlitemountaingear.com/products/junction-40)

### Part B: DCF / Dyneema composites

  - RBTR 1" DCF repair tape: a DCF strip with one-sided adhesive, applied to the inner side and rolled flat; cures at room temperature over several hours. Retailer guidance. [RBTR repair tape](https://ripstopbytheroll.com/products/1-cuben-fiber-repair-tape)

- **Sewing and building:**
  - Keep stitches 3 mm or more from the edge, add glue on film-both-sides grades, tape seams, and cut with micro-serrated scissors. Retailer guidance. [Extremtextil, DCF 0.96](https://www.extremtextil.de/en/dyneema-composite-fabric-0.96-33g-sqm/71711)

- **UV:** UV-resistant. Retailer and maker claim. [Extremtextil](https://www.extremtextil.de/en/know-how/infos-on-dyneema-composites)
- **Availability to home makers:** good for plain DCF and Hybrids (RBTR, Dutchware, Extremtextil and others), though many RBTR grades are sold out. Woven Composite is not available before 2027. [Extremtextil](https://www.extremtextil.de/en/know-how/infos-on-dyneema-composites)
