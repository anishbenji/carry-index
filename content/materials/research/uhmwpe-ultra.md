## Findings

### 1. UHMWPE as a fibre

**What it is, molecular weight and gel spinning**
- UHMWPE is linear polyethylene with a molecular weight of "several (usually 3–6) million Da". The paper equates this with a degree of polymerisation above about 50,000. [Malkin et al., *Polymers* 16(24) 3501, Dec 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4360/16/24/3501)
- The ASTM resin standard does not define UHMW-PE by molecular weight. It defines it as linear ethylene polymers with a relative viscosity of 1.44 or greater (ASTM D4020-18, active, last updated 30 Aug 2018). The full standard is paywalled; only the abstract was read. [ASTM D4020-18](https://store.astm.org/d4020-18.html)
- Avient (the Dyneema owner) gives chain length in atoms, not daltons. A Dyneema chain typically has about 100,000 carbon atoms, against about 10,000 in a plastic bag. [Dyneema: The science of Dyneema](https://www.dyneema.com/experience-more/the-science-of-dyneema)
- Gel spinning, per Avient:
  - the polymer is dissolved and heated in an extruder;
  - a proprietary gel-spinning process disentangles the molecules;
  - the dissolved polymer is forced through a plate with many small holes to form fibre;
  - a draw cycle then further aligns the molecules into strands.
  
  [Dyneema: The science of Dyneema](https://www.dyneema.com/experience-more/the-science-of-dyneema)
- Carryology gives the same outline: the polymer is dissolved into a gel with a solvent, then the gel strands are stretched to align the molecules. [Carryology, "The Pro Guide to Dyneema", 22 Apr 2021](https://www.carryology.com/liking/industry/the-pro-guide-to-dyneema-the-modern-day-miracle-material/)
- **Why long chains give strength:** the makers' pages put it down to alignment. Long, disentangled chains are drawn parallel so that load runs along the chains. A more detailed mechanism (for example, chain overlap sharing load) was not found in a source opened this session, so it is unsourced here. Challenge's own page describes UHMWPE as gel-spun but gives no mechanism. [Dyneema science page](https://www.dyneema.com/experience-more/the-science-of-dyneema), [Challenge UltraWeave](https://www.challenge-outdoor.com/ultra-weave)

**Density**
- 0.975 g/cm³ (given as specific gravity), from a MiniFIBERS technical data sheet for Dyneema SK crimped staple. The sheet names Dyneema as a trademark of Avient Protective Materials and is undated (ref. DCN 260601). [MiniFIBERS PI-TDS Dyneema SK](https://www.minifibers.com/files/TD/PI-TDS_Dyneema-SK-Crimped-Staple.pdf)
- DSM's SK75 brochure says only that the density is less than one. Avient says the fibre is light enough to float on water. [DSM Dyneema SK75 brochure (hosted by Pelican Rope)](https://pelicanrope.com/content/PDFs/DyneemaSK75_Tech_Sheet.pdf), [Avient Techtextil 2026 release](https://www.avient.com/es/node/25920)

**Strength compared with steel: what the fibre makers actually say**
- **DSM (SK75 brochure, undated):** "On a weight-for-weight basis, it is up to 15 times stronger than steel". The same sentence says up to 40% stronger than aramid. Note the words "up to" and the weight-for-weight basis. The brochure does not say which steel. [DSM Dyneema SK75 brochure](https://pelicanrope.com/content/PDFs/DyneemaSK75_Tech_Sheet.pdf)
- **Avient (press release, 15 Apr 2026):** "15 times stronger than steel by weight yet light enough to float on water". [Avient Techtextil 2026 release](https://www.avient.com/es/node/25920)
- **Dyneema homepage (©2025 Avient):** "15 times stronger than steel, yet light enough to float on water". No basis is stated on this page. [dyneema.com](https://www.dyneema.com/)
- **Solstice (Spectra):** "Fifteen times stronger than steel by weight". No test basis is given. [Solstice Spectra fibers](https://www.solstice.com/us/en/products/spectra-fibers)
- **Challenge:** says UHMWPE's tensile strength is 15× that of steel at the same weight. This is a maker claim with no method. [Challenge UltraWeave](https://www.challenge-outdoor.com/ultra-weave)
- **Underlying fibre data (DSM SK75):**
  - tenacity 35.1 cN/dtex (39.8 g/den) for 1760 dtex yarn;
  - modulus 1160 cN/dtex (113 GPa);
  - elongation at break 3.5%, dry and wet.
  
  [DSM SK75 brochure](https://pelicanrope.com/content/PDFs/DyneemaSK75_Tech_Sheet.pdf)
- **MiniFIBERS sheet for Dyneema SK staple:**
  - tenacity about 32–38 g/den, about 3.0 GPa;
  - modulus about 104 GPa;
  - elongation under 4.0%.
  
  [MiniFIBERS PI-TDS](https://www.minifibers.com/files/TD/PI-TDS_Dyneema-SK-Crimped-Staple.pdf)

**Weaknesses relevant to bags**
- **Low melting point:**
  - DSM gives a melting point between 144 °C and 152 °C. [DSM SK75 brochure](https://pelicanrope.com/content/PDFs/DyneemaSK75_Tech_Sheet.pdf)
  - MiniFIBERS gives 144–152 °C (290–306 °F). [MiniFIBERS PI-TDS](https://www.minifibers.com/files/TD/PI-TDS_Dyneema-SK-Crimped-Staple.pdf)
  - A 2024 paper puts it at about 140 °C. [Malkin et al. 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4360/16/24/3501)
- **Working-temperature limit:**
  - DSM gives a use range of −150 °C to 70 °C. It says brief exposure above that causes no serious loss of properties. [DSM SK75 brochure](https://pelicanrope.com/content/PDFs/DyneemaSK75_Tech_Sheet.pdf)
  - MiniFIBERS recommends continuous use at 70 °C or lower. [MiniFIBERS PI-TDS](https://www.minifibers.com/files/TD/PI-TDS_Dyneema-SK-Crimped-Staple.pdf)
- **Heat for HMPE line:** a rigging guide hosted by US Sailing calls heat "HMPE's one mechanical weakness". It notes melting at about 144 °C and warns about heat guns. The guide is by Evans Starzinger, revised 22 Jan 2014. [Dyneema/Spectra Lifelines (US Sailing PDF)](https://www.ussailing.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Dyneema-Spectra-Lifeline-Revision-Jan-2014.pdf)
- **Creep:**
  - DSM says the fibre elongates irreversibly under sustained load and temperature. It claims Dyneema has the lowest creep among commercial HMPE fibres but gives no rate. [DSM SK75 brochure](https://pelicanrope.com/content/PDFs/DyneemaSK75_Tech_Sheet.pdf)
  - Samson Rope defines creep as time-dependent, irreversible elongation under constant long-term load. For rope at 20% of rated break strength, it estimates SK75 creeps about 0.1% per month and SK78 about a third of that. This is a rope-maker estimate, from a bulletin of about March 2012. [Samson Rope, Understanding Creep](https://www.samsonrope.com/resources/general/understanding-creep)
  - No source in this session measures creep in bag fabric. The relevance to bags (for example, sag under constant strap load) is unconfirmed.
- **Low surface energy (hard to glue or bond):** a 2011 Drexel thesis attributes poor adhesion to resins to "chemical inertness, poor wettability and low surface energy of the fiber". [Yim, Drexel thesis 2011](https://researchdiscovery.drexel.edu/esploro/outputs/doctoral/Tailoring-interfacial-performance-of-UHMW-PE/991014632395004721)
- **Hard to dye:** a 2021 journal paper says the fibre "cannot be dyed using conventional dyeing techniques". It gives the reasons as strong hydrophobicity and no polar groups. [Farooq et al., Mehran Univ. Res. J. Eng. Tech. 40(4), 2021](https://publications.muet.edu.pk/index.php/muetrj/article/view/2283)
  - Relevance to ULTRA: TX Ultra's polyester face is described as suited to dye-sub printing. Extremtextil lists ULTRA TEN X (100% UHMWPE face, "dyed") in six colours. Coloured UHMWPE yarn therefore exists, but no source in this session explains how it is coloured. [Challenge TX Ultra](https://www.challenge-outdoor.com/tx-ultra), [Extremtextil ULTRA TEN X](https://www.extremtextil.de/en/ultra-ten-x-backpack-laminate-with-uhmwpe-99-sqm/73200)
- **Printing:** no source found on printing directly onto UHMWPE. Not searched further.
- **UV behaviour:**
  - DSM describes the fibre as resistant to UV, but gives no figure (qualitative claim only). [DSM SK75 brochure](https://pelicanrope.com/content/PDFs/DyneemaSK75_Tech_Sheet.pdf)
  - MiniFIBERS claims excellent resistance to sunlight and UV, with no data. [MiniFIBERS PI-TDS](https://www.minifibers.com/files/TD/PI-TDS_Dyneema-SK-Crimped-Staple.pdf)
  - The US Sailing-hosted guide calls HMPE one of the most UV-resistant fibres. It still reports that line strength drops fastest in the first ~18 months, losing about 20–35% after 24 months of continuous sun. This is for lifeline rope, not fabric. [US Sailing PDF](https://www.ussailing.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Dyneema-Spectra-Lifeline-Revision-Jan-2014.pdf)
- **Moisture:** DSM lists moisture regain as none (versus about 4.5% for nylon) and water take-up when soaked as none. [DSM SK75 brochure](https://pelicanrope.com/content/PDFs/DyneemaSK75_Tech_Sheet.pdf)
- **Washing and drying heat limits:**
  - No UHMWPE fibre maker states a laundering temperature. The 70 °C use limit and the 144–152 °C melt range above are the only fibre-level numbers.
  - For Dyneema Composite Fabric (a laminate, not ULTRA), Zpacks says to hand-wash in warm water, air-dry, and never use a dryer, because heat may cause shrinkage. This is bag-maker guidance. [Zpacks DCF care](https://www.zpacks.com/blogs/care-instructions/how-to-wash-dyneema®-composite-fabric-gear)

### 2. Fibre brands and owners now

- **Dyneema → Avient.**
  - Avient completed its purchase of DSM's protective materials business "including the Dyneema brand" on 1 Sep 2022. The business was renamed Avient Protective Materials. [Avient 8-K Ex. 99.1, 1 Sep 2022](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1122976/000119312522235810/d355900dex991.htm), [Textile World, 1 Sep 2022](https://www.textileworld.com/textile-world/fiber-world/2022/09/avient-completes-acquisition-of-dsm-protective-materials-dyneema)
  - The deal was announced on 20 Apr 2022 at about $1.48 billion. [PlasticsToday, 21 Apr 2022](https://www.plasticstoday.com/materials/avient-buys-dsm-protective-materials-in-1-5-billion-deal)
  - Dyneema's own "About us" timeline says DSM:
    - first produced the material in an experiment in the 1960s;
    - patented gel spinning in the 1970s;
    - commercialised the fibre as Dyneema in the 1980s.
  - The same timeline says Avient acquired Dyneema in 2022. It lists plants in Greenville NC, Mesa AZ, Heerlen (NL), Laiwu (CN) and Tsuruga (JP). [Dyneema About us](https://www.dyneema.com/experience-more/about-us)
- **Spectra → Solstice Advanced Materials.**
  - Honeywell completed the spin-off of Solstice Advanced Materials (Nasdaq: SOLS) on 30 Oct 2025. [Honeywell release, 30 Oct 2025](https://www.honeywell.com/us/en/press/2025/10/honeywell-completes-spin-off-of-solstice-advanced-materials)
  - Solstice's closing release lists Spectra® among its brands. [Solstice 8-K Ex. 99.1, 30 Oct 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2064953/000162828025047305/exhibit991-closing8xk.htm)
  - Solstice's Spectra product page says the company was formed through the 2025 spin-off of Honeywell's former Advanced Materials business (page ©2026). [Solstice Spectra fibers](https://www.solstice.com/us/en/products/spectra-fibers)
- **Challenge's fibre: unbranded.**
  - Garage Grown Gear, quoting Challenge Outdoor's then managing director Hale Walcoff: "the fibers used in Ultra are unbranded". The same article says ULTRA is made in one of Challenge's factories in China. [Garage Grown Gear, 14 Mar 2022](https://www.garagegrowngear.com/blogs/trail-talk/ultra-fabric-ultralight-cottage-gear)
  - Carryology describes the fibre as Challenge Sailcloth's own U-PE fibre. [Carryology, 2 Nov 2022](https://www.carryology.com/materials/challenge-sailcloth-the-dawn-of-ultra-fabrics/)
  - Challenge's current pages (UltraWeave, FAQ) say only "Ultra-PE" or "U-PE" and name no supplier. The 2022 "unbranded" statement has not been restated in 2026 material seen here. [Challenge UltraWeave](https://www.challenge-outdoor.com/ultra-weave), [Challenge FAQ](https://www.challenge-outdoor.com/faq)
  - Rockywoods' UltraGrid listing says Ultra fibre is UHMWPE "also sold as Spectra™ or Dyneema™". This is a retailer's generic description, not a sourcing statement. [Rockywoods UltraGrid](https://rockywoods.com/products/ultragrid)

### 3. Challenge ULTRA: the current line

**Where the data lives (Oct 2026)**
- Challenge's ULTRA pages are on challenge-outdoor.com. No ULTRA data-sheet PDF is linked anywhere on the site. The only PDFs are an ECOPAK features sheet (2021) and an RBC tech guide (2022). The specs below come from the product pages. [UltraWeave](https://www.challenge-outdoor.com/ultra-weave), [UltraWeave Collection](https://www.challenge-outdoor.com/ultraweave-collection), [TX Ultra](https://www.challenge-outdoor.com/tx-ultra)
- None of the Challenge pages shows a publication or revision date. Image upload timestamps on the UltraWeave page point to about Feb 2022, and on TX Ultra to about Oct 2022. Challenge does not state test conditions (wheel, load, endpoint, sample count) anywhere.
- Every Challenge abrasion figure is written "ASTM 3884". This is presumably ASTM D3884, the Taber rotary-platform double-head method. No wheel, load or endpoint is given. All tear figures are ASTM D2261 (tongue tear), in lb.

**Generations: what "X" and "TX" mean (settled from Challenge's own pages, plus retailers and makers for dates)**
- **Gen-1 ULTRA (about 2021–2022), now discontinued:**
  - woven UHMWPE/polyester face on a 0.5 mil RUV™ film;
  - no cross-ply;
  - weights: ULTRA 100 99 g/m², 200 119 g/m², 400 158 g/m², 800 288 g/m².
  
  [Extremtextil ULTRA hub (discontinued table)](https://www.extremtextil.de/en/challengeultra)
- **"X" (UltraWeave Ultra100X / 200X / 400X / 800X):** the woven face plus an "Ultra-PE CrossPly" plus a 0.75 mil film backing. The cross-ply is described two ways:
  - Ripstop by the Roll: a thin black Ultra cross-ply yarn at 45° to the warp, added for bias strength and longevity. [RBTR ULTRA 800X](https://ripstopbytheroll.com/collections/laminate-composite-fabrics/products/challenge-ultra-800)
  - Extremtextil: black fibres in a diamond pattern between the plies. [Extremtextil ULTRA 400 X](https://www.extremtextil.de/en/ultra-400-x-backpack-laminate-with-uhmwpe-178g-sqm/72683)
  
  [UltraWeave Collection](https://www.challenge-outdoor.com/ultraweave-collection)
- Challenge never spells out what the letter "X" stands for. Extremtextil says the X variant "is thus more dimensionally stable and longer waterproof" because of its thicker film. [Extremtextil ULTRA 400 X](https://www.extremtextil.de/en/ultra-400-x-backpack-laminate-with-uhmwpe-178g-sqm/72683)
- **When X arrived:**
  - Pack makers discussed UltraX and UltraTX as new in Feb 2023 (a forum, so treat as maker or owner reports).
  - One maker said UltraX adds a cross-ply for bias strength.
  - MLD's Ron Bell said it adds more reinforcement against diagonal stretch, plus a thicker laminate with a different glue bond.
  
  [BPL thread, Feb–Jun 2023](https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/challenge-ultra-new-variations-ultrax-ultratx/)
- Adventure Alan calls UltraX a 2023 upgrade that thickened the film from 0.5 to 0.75. It writes "mm", which is almost certainly meant as mil. The same piece says the upgrade added about 0.35 oz/yd². [Adventure Alan, updated 9 May 2023](https://www.adventurealan.com/what-is-challenge-ultra-fabric/)
- **"TX" in the UltraWeave line (Ultra200TX, Ultra400TX, Ultra800TX):** the X build plus a 70d recycled polyester ripstop bonded behind the film. Challenge's description: "The technical properties of Ultra200X, plus a ripstop backing for improved durability and stitch-holding". Challenge lists the **same tear and abrasion values** for each TX as for its X. TX is therefore not "tuned" for tear or abrasion; the backer is there for durability and stitch-holding. [UltraWeave Collection](https://www.challenge-outdoor.com/ultraweave-collection)
- **"TX Ultra™" (TX50, TX70) is a different product:**
  - It is described as an "ultralight 3-layer laminate designed for small bags and accessories". [TX Ultra](https://www.challenge-outdoor.com/tx-ultra)
  - The face is polyester ripstop, not UHMWPE, with a 45° Ultra CrossPly and a 0.25 mil PET film.
  - The site should not confuse "TX Ultra" with the "…TX" UltraWeave styles.

- **Sailing:**
  - multiple World and National Championships;
  - a Collegiate National Championship;
  - a Pan Am Games gold medal;
  - Tufts University Athletic Hall of Fame.
  
  Neither obituary gives classes or years. [Sailing Scuttlebutt](https://www.sailingscuttlebutt.com/2023/10/06/eight-bells-hale-walcoff/)
- **Career:**
  - The obituaries list North Sails, Goetz Custom Boats, New England Ropes, GMT Composites and Dimension-Polyant, with no titles or dates.
  - He is described as managing director of Challenge Outdoor.
  
  [Points East](https://www.pointseast.com/?p=31260)
- **Dimension-Polyant and X-Pac:** Carryology credits him with work behind Dimension-Polyant's fabrics. On its 20+ X-Pac styles it says "it's likely Hale designed it". No job title or years are given, so the deep-dive's "spent years developing many X-Pac variants" is only partly supported. [Carryology, 2 Nov 2022](https://www.carryology.com/materials/challenge-sailcloth-the-dawn-of-ultra-fabrics/)

  - Carryology: after a sabbatical he pitched a recycled-polyester laminate pilot to North Sails. That pilot was shut down in early 2020. He restarted it with Challenge Sailcloth's support and became managing director of Challenge Outdoor, Challenge Sailcloth's softgoods arm. [Carryology](https://www.carryology.com/materials/challenge-sailcloth-the-dawn-of-ultra-fabrics/)
  - Backpacker: he began the all-recycled fabric work with Challenge Sailcloth in 2019. ECOPAK was the first product and ULTRA followed. [Backpacker, Apr 2022](https://www.backpacker.com/gear/is-ultra-the-new-dyneema/?scope=anon)
  - Ripstop by the Roll's episode 36 page calls him "Managing Director of Challenge Sailcloth". Other sources say Challenge Outdoor. [RBTR Ep. 36](https://ripstopbytheroll.com/pages/ep-36-challenge-sailcloth)

### 5. Field reports

**Miyagi on the Trail torture test**
- **Video:** "Can I Destroy the New Waymark Gear "EVLV Ultra" Backpack?", channel Miyagi on the Trail, premiered 27 Mar 2022. [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YU2IoK_FvE4) (short link: https://youtu.be/YU2IoK_FvE4)
- **Fabric (from the description):** "Ecopac Ultra 200 and Ultra 400 material". The description does not say which panels use which. The premiere date means this is **gen-1 ULTRA (0.5 mil film, no cross-ply), not today's X**. [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YU2IoK_FvE4)
- **Tests listed in the description:**
  - frozen in an 8-inch block of ice for 10 days;
  - dropped, still frozen in the block, from 50 ft onto concrete and brick;
  - dragged 2 miles through mud, rocks and debris;
  - washed on the maximum cycle in hot water for 155 minutes;
  - dried on high heat for 98 minutes.
  
  [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YU2IoK_FvE4)
- **Results:** the description does not state them. It only tells viewers to watch. The outcome was not confirmed from text in this session.
- Waymark's own site blocked fetching, so the pack's panel layout is not confirmed.

**Other wear reports**
- **Owner reports (BPL forum, Jan 2026):**
  - About three years of Ultra 100/200/400 on an SWD Long Haul, "proven plenty durable".
  - An Ultra400X pack that has held up very well.
  - One owner found a Kakwa in Ultra200 less bombproof than expected.
  - Another expects 400X to soften as it breaks in.
  
  [BPL: considering SWD Long Haul in Ultra400X](https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/considering-swd-long-haul-in-ultra400x/)
- **Owner reports, sceptical (BPL forum, May 2025):**
  - One desert hiker says its abrasion resistance "has not impressed me compared to the lowly Cordura".
  - Another cites a handful of anecdotal reports of real-world abrasion falling short of the lab numbers.
  
  [BPL: rough off-trail fabric](https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/which-pack-fabric-is-best-for-rough-off-trail-hiking-scrambling/)
- **Delamination reports (gen-1 fabric):**
  - Owner, relayed (BPL, Jan 2023): a KS Ultralight pack in Ultra 200 delaminated after a PCT thru-hike of about 2,500 miles. It was at seams and also mid-panel, per a Reddit report relayed on BPL.
  - Other PCT owners reported abrasion but no rips.
  - One owner later saw delamination at the top of an UltraWeave pack at 300 miles.
  
  [BPL: Ultra 200 delamination after PCT](https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/yet-another-story-of-ultra-200-delamination-after-a-pct-thru)
- **Maker reports on delamination and backers (forum, Jun 2023):**
  - MLD's Ron Bell said some regular-Ultra packs showed delamination after a year, with no structural failures, and that UltraX resists delamination better.
  - Nunatak said the TX ripstop backer on samples peeled off easily.
  - Syoten reported weak seams after bar-tacking TX with a 50d backer.
  
  [BPL UltraX/UltraTX thread](https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/challenge-ultra-new-variations-ultrax-ultratx/)
- **Maker (ULA, Mar 2025):** ULA stopped seam-taping because the tape "is prone to become delaminated". It says Robic is more easily repaired than Ultra. [ULA blog, 5 Mar 2025](https://www.ula-equipment.com/2025/03/05/whats-the-difference-between-ultra-and-robic-fabrics)
- **Maker (LiteAF, Mar 2022):** two Ultra 200 packs with thousands of miles. The mesh failed first while the Ultra was holding up very well. In the same article, a reader reported a golf-ball-sized hole after towing a pack less than two miles (owner comment). [Garage Grown Gear](https://www.garagegrowngear.com/blogs/trail-talk/ultra-fabric-ultralight-cottage-gear)
- **Reviewer (SectionHiker, Aug 2025):**
  - Ultra slides over rock and bark without fuzzing as quickly as nylon.
  - Repeated setting down on sharp rock will eventually wear it through.
  - Film wear shows first at edges and hard creases.
  
  [SectionHiker, Ultra fabrics](https://sectionhiker.com/ultra-fabrics-for-ultralight-backpacking-gear-tents-packs-and-stuff-sacks/)
- **Reviewer (Pack Hacker, Aer Ultra Sling in Ultra200X, updated 11 Aug 2026):** after one year the sling "still looks new". [Pack Hacker](https://www.packhacker.com/travel-gear/aer/ultra-sling-pack-hacker-aer-collab/)
- **Reviewer (Adventure Alan, 2023):** no tears or damage after over a year of testing. [Adventure Alan](https://www.adventurealan.com/what-is-challenge-ultra-fabric/)
- **Reviewer (SectionHiker, Dec 2023, SWD Rugged Long Haul in Ultra 400):** the reviewer claims it is far more abrasion-resistant than DCF. Readers commented that it still looked perfect after scree. [SectionHiker SWD review](https://sectionhiker.com/superior-wilderness-designs-rugged-long-haul-50-backpack-review/)
- **Gaps:**
  - No specific reports of **pilling**, or of **colour wear** (for example, white fibre showing through Black Magic), were found.
  - One owner says white ULTRA takes on a patina with dirt. [BPL SWD thread](https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/considering-swd-long-haul-in-ultra400x/)

## Deep-dive claims checked

**UHMWPE fibre**
- **UHMWPE is millions of daltons:** CONFIRMED. Usually 3–6 million Da. [Malkin et al. 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4360/16/24/3501)
- **A plastic bag is tens of thousands of daltons:** UNCONFIRMED. Avient gives only about 10,000 atoms per chain for bag PE, not a dalton figure. [Dyneema science](https://www.dyneema.com/experience-more/the-science-of-dyneema)
- **One dalton is about one hydrogen atom:** not checked (outside this file's sources).
- **Gram for gram stronger than steel:** CONFIRMED as a maker claim. DSM says "up to 15×" on a weight-for-weight basis; Avient and Solstice say 15× "by weight". No maker names the steel grade or a test basis. [DSM SK75](https://pelicanrope.com/content/PDFs/DyneemaSK75_Tech_Sheet.pdf), [Avient 2026](https://www.avient.com/es/node/25920), [Solstice](https://www.solstice.com/us/en/products/spectra-fibers)
- **Doesn't absorb water; wet stays the same weight and strength:** PARTLY. DSM lists moisture regain and water take-up as none, and the same elongation dry and wet. A wet-strength figure was not found. [DSM SK75](https://pelicanrope.com/content/PDFs/DyneemaSK75_Tech_Sheet.pdf)
- **Density 0.97 g/cm³:** CONFIRMED (0.975 g/cm³). [MiniFIBERS](https://www.minifibers.com/files/TD/PI-TDS_Dyneema-SK-Crimped-Staple.pdf)
- **Nylon 1.14 and polyester 1.38 g/cm³:** not checked here; belongs to the nylon and polyester files.

**Brands and owners**
- **"Dyneema is DSM's brand":** WRONG (out of date). Avient has owned it since 1 Sep 2022. [Avient 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1122976/000119312522235810/d355900dex991.htm)
- **DSM "producing UHMWPE since the 1970s":** PARTLY.
  - Avient's timeline: DSM experiment in the 1960s, gel-spinning patent in the 1970s, commercialised as Dyneema in the 1980s.
  - Carryology says it was established commercially by 1990.
  
  [Dyneema About us](https://www.dyneema.com/experience-more/about-us), [Carryology](https://www.carryology.com/liking/industry/the-pro-guide-to-dyneema-the-modern-day-miracle-material/)
- **Dyneema "dominates the market":** UNCONFIRMED. No market-share source was found.
- **Spectra is now made by Solstice, spun off from Honeywell in 2025:** CONFIRMED (30 Oct 2025). [Honeywell](https://www.honeywell.com/us/en/press/2025/10/honeywell-completes-spin-off-of-solstice-advanced-materials), [Solstice 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2064953/000162828025047305/exhibit991-closing8xk.htm)
- **Challenge uses non-branded UHMWPE:** CONFIRMED, per Walcoff in 2022. It has not been restated on current Challenge pages. [Garage Grown Gear](https://www.garagegrowngear.com/blogs/trail-talk/ultra-fabric-ultralight-cottage-gear)
- **ULTRA is by Challenge Outdoor, the soft-goods division of Challenge Sailcloth:** CONFIRMED. [Carryology](https://www.carryology.com/materials/challenge-sailcloth-the-dawn-of-ultra-fabrics/)

**ULTRA 400X build**
- **Three layers:** CONFIRMED. The layers are:
  - 400d UHMWPE/high-tenacity polyester face;
  - Ultra-PE CrossPly;
  - 0.75 mil film.
  
  [UltraWeave Collection](https://www.challenge-outdoor.com/ultraweave-collection)
- **Polyester adds shape retention; UHMWPE gives strength and abrasion:** UNCONFIRMED. Challenge gives no role split. Extremtextil attributes abrasion and tear to the 67% UHMWPE share. [Extremtextil hub](https://www.extremtextil.de/en/challengeultra)
- **The cross-ply runs at angles, spreads load and keeps the bag's shape:** PARTLY.
  - The cross-ply is at 45° (Challenge TX, RBTR) or in a diamond pattern (Extremtextil).
  - It was added for bias strength and longevity (RBTR) and against diagonal stretch (MLD, forum).
  - "Keeps the bag's shape from sagging over time" is not stated by any source.
  
  [RBTR 800X](https://ripstopbytheroll.com/collections/laminate-composite-fabrics/products/challenge-ultra-800), [BPL thread](https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/challenge-ultra-new-variations-ultrax-ultratx/)
- **0.75 mil UV-resistant recycled polyester film:** CONFIRMED (0.75 mil for X; recycled PET; described as UV-stable). [Extremtextil 400 X](https://www.extremtextil.de/en/ultra-400-x-backpack-laminate-with-uhmwpe-178g-sqm/72683)
- **"RUV = Recycled UV-resistant":** UNCONFIRMED. Challenge never expands RUV. It calls the film recycled, and the 2021 sheet calls it "97% UV resistant". [ECOPAK PDF](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/62018e9b29f2cd0fc23c3a0c/t/6206d8d1a7394424ce0818e4/1644615893252/2021_EcoPak-Features_v8.pdf)
- **400TX adds a thin 70D polyester ripstop backing:** CONFIRMED. Challenge specifies a 70d recycled polyester ripstop. ULA describes its 400TX backing as white taffeta. [UltraWeave Collection](https://www.challenge-outdoor.com/ultraweave-collection)

- **Sailing world champion:** CONFIRMED (multiple World Championships; classes and years not given). [Scuttlebutt](https://www.sailingscuttlebutt.com/2023/10/06/eight-bells-hale-walcoff/)

[Carryology](https://www.carryology.com/materials/challenge-sailcloth-the-dawn-of-ultra-fabrics/), [Backpacker](https://www.backpacker.com/gear/is-ultra-the-new-dyneema/?scope=anon)

## In use

**Hand-feel, sound and crinkle**
- *Reviewer:* SectionHiker says ULTRA is stiffer and crisper than nylon with less drape. It can rustle (quieter than thin DCF) and is "not silent like soft nylon". [SectionHiker, Aug 2025](https://sectionhiker.com/ultra-fabrics-for-ultralight-backpacking-gear-tents-packs-and-stuff-sacks/)
- *Maker:* ULA says Ultra is stiffer than nylon. [ULA blog](https://www.ula-equipment.com/2025/03/05/whats-the-difference-between-ultra-and-robic-fabrics)
- *Owner (RBTR reviews of 200X):* "Nice look and feel"; another reviewer found it light and very pliable. [RBTR 200X](https://ripstopbytheroll.com/products/challenge-ultra-200)
- *Owner (RBTR reviews of 400):* stiff and harder to sew than lighter fabrics. [RBTR 400](https://ripstopbytheroll.com/products/challenge-ultra-400)
- *Owner (BPL forum, Jan 2026):*
  - One owner prefers Ultra200's feel and compressibility over 400.
  - Another expects 400X to soften as it breaks in.
  
  [BPL SWD thread](https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/considering-swd-long-haul-in-ultra400x/)

**Creasing and ageing**
- *Retailer guidance:* Extremtextil warns that fabric shipped folded gets permanent fold marks, which it says do not affect technical properties. It offers roll shipping to avoid them. [Extremtextil 400 X](https://www.extremtextil.de/en/ultra-400-x-backpack-laminate-with-uhmwpe-178g-sqm/72683)
- *Reviewer:* film wear shows first at edges and hard creases. SectionHiker advises rolling loosely and not folding on the same line. [SectionHiker](https://sectionhiker.com/ultra-fabrics-for-ultralight-backpacking-gear-tents-packs-and-stuff-sacks/)
- *Owner:* white ULTRA picks up a patina with dirt. [BPL SWD thread](https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/considering-swd-long-haul-in-ultra400x/)

**How it takes abrasion**
- *Maker figures:* Taber cycles run from 3,600 (100X) to 16,000+ (800X), with method details missing (see the data table).
- *Reviewer:* SectionHiker says it fuzzes less quickly than nylon and will eventually wear through on sharp rock. [SectionHiker](https://sectionhiker.com/ultra-fabrics-for-ultralight-backpacking-gear-tents-packs-and-stuff-sacks/)
- *Owner:* mixed. Several owners say it is durable over 1–3 years. Some desert and off-trail users report abrasion below expectations. [BPL SWD thread](https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/considering-swd-long-haul-in-ultra400x/), [BPL off-trail thread](https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/which-pack-fabric-is-best-for-rough-off-trail-hiking-scrambling/)

**Delamination**
- *Owner and maker reports:* reports exist for gen-1 ULTRA 200 (thru-hike mileage). MLD (forum, 2023) says UltraX holds up better. [BPL delamination](https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/yet-another-story-of-ultra-200-delamination-after-a-pct-thru), [BPL UltraX](https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/challenge-ultra-new-variations-ultrax-ultratx/)
- *Maker test (Pa'lante, gen-1 400, undated):* no delamination after all three of these steps:
  - 12 h at −12 °C, then crumpling;
  - a 40-min wash;
  - 1 h in a dryer on high.
  
  This is the maker's own home test, not a lab. [Pa'lante](https://palantepacks.com/Ultra-Weave-Specs)

**Cleaning**
- *Challenge:* no care or cleaning guidance found on its FAQ or product pages. [Challenge FAQ](https://www.challenge-outdoor.com/faq)
- *Reviewer:* rinse with cool water, spot-clean with mild soap and a soft sponge, and air-dry away from high heat. No machine washing and no harsh solvents. [SectionHiker](https://sectionhiker.com/ultra-fabrics-for-ultralight-backpacking-gear-tents-packs-and-stuff-sacks/)
- *Reviewer:* the Ultra200X sling wipes clean easily. [Pack Hacker](https://www.packhacker.com/travel-gear/aer/ultra-sling-pack-hacker-aer-collab/)
- *Bag-maker guidance for ULTRA bags:* none found. Aer's FAQ failed to load, and search ran out. DCF guidance from Zpacks (no dryer, because heat can shrink it) is the nearest maker parallel, but DCF is not ULTRA. [Zpacks](https://www.zpacks.com/blogs/care-instructions/how-to-wash-dyneema®-composite-fabric-gear)

**Heat and UV limits**
- *Fibre maker:*
  - use range up to 70 °C;
  - melts at 144–152 °C;
  - so keep it away from high-heat dryers, irons and heat guns. This advice is an inference from fibre data, not ULTRA-specific guidance.
  
  [DSM SK75](https://pelicanrope.com/content/PDFs/DyneemaSK75_Tech_Sheet.pdf)
- *Torture test (gen-1):* Miyagi's dryer run was 98 min on high heat. The outcome was not confirmed from text. [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YU2IoK_FvE4)
- *Maker:* the film is UV-stable (Extremtextil). The RUV film is "97% UV resistant" (2021 Challenge sheet; method not stated). [Extremtextil 100 X](https://www.extremtextil.de/en/ultra-100-x-backpack-laminate-with-uhmwpe-112g-sqm/72681), [ECOPAK PDF](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/62018e9b29f2cd0fc23c3a0c/t/6206d8d1a7394424ce0818e4/1644615893252/2021_EcoPak-Features_v8.pdf)

**Repair**
- *Maker:* ULTRA 100 PSA (Challenge) and ULTRA 100 X PSA (Extremtextil) are sold for repair patches, seam tape and bar-tack reinforcement. [UltraWeave Collection](https://www.challenge-outdoor.com/ultraweave-collection), [Extremtextil 100 X PSA](https://www.extremtextil.de/en/ultra-100-x-psa-with-adhesive-backing-with-uhmwpe/72684)
- *Maker:* UltraTNT™ PSA Tape is a "waterproof seam & repair tape". It comes in ⅝ in, 1¼ in (minimum for waterproof seams), 2, 3 and 4 in, and goes on the film side. Challenge's application steps:
  - work at 70 °F or warmer;
  - clean the surface with rubbing alcohol;
  - roll the tape on firmly;
  - let it cure for 24 h.
  
  [Challenge UltraTNT PSA Tape](https://www.challenge-outdoor.com/ultratnt-psa-tape)
- *Maker:* ULA says Robic is more easily repaired than Ultra, and offers in-house repairs. [ULA blog](https://www.ula-equipment.com/2025/03/05/whats-the-difference-between-ultra-and-robic-fabrics)
- *Owner (MYOG forum):* glue does not bond well to woven UHMWPE. No home method was confirmed to work. [BPL bonding thread, Jan 2023](https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/bonding-to-woven-uhmwpe-fabric-aka-a-real-pita/)

**Sewing and making (MYOG)**
- *Maker guidance:* reinforce high-load points with an extra fabric layer. This applies especially to TX Ultra and ULTRA X (shoulder-strap anchors, daisy chains). [Challenge TX Ultra](https://www.challenge-outdoor.com/tx-ultra), [Extremtextil TEN X](https://www.extremtextil.de/en/ultra-ten-x-backpack-laminate-with-uhmwpe-99-sqm/73200)
- *Owner (RBTR reviews):*
  - use a rotary cutter or snap-off knife rather than scissors (it dulls blades);
  - use a hot knife to seal edges;
  - one sewer used a #16 needle with Mara 40 thread.
  
  [RBTR 400](https://ripstopbytheroll.com/products/challenge-ultra-400), [RBTR 200X](https://ripstopbytheroll.com/products/challenge-ultra-200)
- *Maker:* no ECOPAK or ULTRA fabric is weldable, because there is no TPU film. Seams are sealed with PSA tape. [Challenge FAQ](https://www.challenge-outdoor.com/faq)
